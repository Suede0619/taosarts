# Art as Teacher Foundation: mission, audience and a useful approach

**Checked October 25, 2026 against [the foundation's own website](https://artasteacher.org/).** Published facts and the proposals below remain separate. No contact, partnership or contribution has occurred.

## What the foundation publishes

Art as Teacher says it began in Taos in **2025**. It presents contemporary Northern New Mexico art through artists, their knowledge and public learning. The page describes organizational development now and first events plus strategic planning in **2027**.

The published founding board includes **Larry Bell, Ron Cooper, Sandra Lerner, Gus Foster, Alexandra Benjamin, Dora and Carl Dillistone, Michael Straus, and Jane McFadden**. It credits **Janet Webb** as an initiating inspiration and identifies **J. Matthew Thomas** as consultant and incoming executive director. “Incoming” does not establish that an appointment has already taken effect. These are the website's descriptions, not independent verification of corporate filings or authority.

The page does not establish an open grant program, available commercial budget, event booking or permission for another project to reproduce the art. Do not treat “foundation” as “a source of money for us.” Its public address can receive an appropriate inquiry only after outreach is authorized.

## What this changes in the audience analysis

Longstanding involvement in the local arts should not become a claim that named individuals reject change, dislike commerce or have a particular personality.

This institution is evidence of new organizing among experienced participants. The practical question is what kind of change serves the purpose they have chosen. A proposal about a generic website listing is unlikely to answer that. A small project that preserves an artist's voice, helps people encounter work intelligently or supports a real program could be relevant. That relevance remains a hypothesis to discuss.

Neither admiration for an artist nor familiarity with a board member confers institutional access. A member met at an opening may be present privately. Ask how a future conversation should be arranged. Do not seek an institutional commitment through a social introduction.

## Distinct people to understand

| Role | What to ask about | A possible useful contribution | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding artist | How the work and inquiry should be encountered and described. | An approved presentation of the artist's own questions and process. | Permission to record, reproduce work, use a voice or sell anything. |
| Executive/program lead | The first program priorities, staffing and delivery constraints. | A small scoped digital production task that supports an actual program. | Authority to accept a partnership or allocate funds without the appropriate process. |
| Board/trustee | Mission, public value, institutional credibility and governance. | A concise proposal showing purpose, rights, effort and cost. | That a friendly conversation is board approval. |
| Educator/research collaborator | Interpretation, audiences, accessibility and attribution. | Accurate links, transcripts, captions and an approved route into existing material. | That “art as teacher” means a particular school curriculum or age group. |
| Patron/supporter | The public result they want to sustain. | An honest bounded project with a delivery plan and funding requirement. | Personal wealth, willingness to give or an introduction to other patrons. |

These roles map to SW03, SW07, SW11 and the specific **SW23 institution-building persona** in [the persona document](../outreach/01-Personas-and-Decision-Roles.md). A mission-led operating foundation differs from a grantmaking persona.

## A small proposal that could be worth a conversation

**One artist, one question, one approved digital encounter.** A visitor chooses a question about the work, hears or reads an authorized response, sees correctly credited cleared material and finds the foundation's own next public program. A simple non-AI version should work first.

Possible deliverables include an accessible page, approved text/audio, a transcript, practical event information and links back to authoritative sources. The foundation controls interpretation and publication. An artist approves their contribution and applicable rights. If AI-assisted search later becomes useful, it answers only from that permissioned material and clearly cites it. It does not synthesize a person's voice or invent an artist's answer.

This would be a production proposal to scope and fund, not an unpaid sample expected from an artist or a commitment by the digital builder. It should begin only if the institution identifies a real need and someone accepts the delivery work.

## A natural question at an opening

> “I saw that Art as Teacher is taking shape. What would you most like people to understand through its first programs?”

Listen. If invited to say more:

> “We're exploring ways to connect an art encounter with a useful next step. For a foundation, that might be one small approved digital piece that helps people hear an artist's thinking and find the real program. I'd like to understand whether that would help before proposing anything.”

The first exchange should not ask for a listing, endorsement, donor list, access to artists or introductions to collectors. If the person wants to continue, ask how and when to arrange a short conversation.

## Relation to the commercial service

The [buyer-led art desk](../planning/11-Website-Pivot-Collector-and-Visit-Service.md) could help a visitor find an artist or seller after a public encounter, if the parties want that connection. Keep education, editorial judgment and commercial routing clear. An artwork shown for learning is not necessarily for sale. Institutional support would not endorse every commercial recommendation.

Begin with the foundation's mission and a specific need it identifies. Discuss a commercial connection separately, with its own consent and terms. No names, artwork, logos, recordings or board associations should become promotional assets for Southwest Arts without permission.

## Outstanding questions

Who handles potential program/digital collaborations? What should the first public programs achieve? What materials are already cleared? What existing systems should a contribution support? What staff time, artist pay, budget and approval process would it require? Would a commercial link be inappropriate? The website alone cannot answer these questions.
