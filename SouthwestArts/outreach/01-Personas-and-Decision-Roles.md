# Southwest Arts personas and decision roles

**October 25, 2026. Working hypotheses, not psychological profiles.** These roles come from the existing business plan, website specification, arts research and the new [service proposal](../planning/11-Website-Pivot-Collector-and-Visit-Service.md). One person may occupy several roles. Ask what matters to the actual person. Do not infer attitudes from age, wealth, birthplace or years in Taos.

Two products appear below. **Service** means the proposed buyer-led art and visit desk. **Shows** means a proposed series of properly hosted Southwest art shows, distinct from Denver Energy Center. Neither has an approved operating budget or recruited partner network.

| ID and persona | What this role can decide | Potential value to test | Likely question or risk | Evidence that earns another conversation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SW01. Established gallery principal | Whether the business participates and how sellers/representation work. | Relevant buyer briefs, appropriate appointments and less speculative selling work. | Why another intermediary? Who owns the client relationship? | A strong sample brief, seller-controlled records and a small measured test. |
| SW02. Gallery manager or sales associate | Day-to-day inventory confirmation, appointments and staff workflow. Owner may control terms. | Clear inquiries with budget, timing and usable work links. | Is this another inbox and catalog to maintain? | One simple response path, capped requests and actual time savings. |
| SW03. Independent living artist | Permission for their work, availability, direct selling and participation. | Paid sales, fair credit and funded commissions. | Fees, image use, unpaid labor and payment reliability. | Written limited permission, accurate records and a real payment route. |
| SW04. Represented artist | Their own participation and rights, within representation agreements. | Suitable interest routed through the appropriate seller. | Will this create conflict with the gallery? | Agreed seller routing and no undisclosed competing offer. |
| SW05. Artist cooperative or studio collective | Member-governed participation and shared promotion. | Fair distribution of useful inquiries and accessible shared administration. | Who selects, who does the work and who gets visibility? | Member approval, transparent selection and a modest workload. |
| SW06. Estate, historical-art or edition dealer | Sale authority, documentation and reproduction permissions. | Informed inquiries with precise object/edition context. | False provenance, artist-proceeds claims or inaccurate attribution. | Seller-verified descriptions and a human expert handoff. |
| SW07. Museum curator, educator or director | Interpretation, public programming and institutional participation within delegated authority. | Better-prepared visits, credited context and access to learning. | Commercial use of institutional credibility or collection images. | Accurate context, permission boundaries and no implied endorsement. |
| SW08. Nonprofit arts producer or community arts organizer | Program fit and a possible collaboration, subject to governance/funding. | Paid artist opportunities and public participation. | A competing event, unpaid staffing or mission drift. | A bounded program proposal with labor, access and funding identified. |
| SW09. Taos Pueblo artist or other Indigenous artist | Their own artwork, sales and permitted personal participation. | Buyer respect, correct maker attribution and paid work. | Cultural appropriation and assumptions about shared authority. | Artist-defined terms. Their consent does not authorize another person's work or community access. |
| SW10. Authorized cultural/community representative | Matters actually within their community or institution's authority. | Accurate visitor conduct, appropriate interpretation and community-defined benefit. | Commercializing cultural material or treating living culture as a product. | An invitation to listen and a clear permission process. No sales pitch substitutes for it. |
| SW11. Longstanding patron, trustee or local connector | Personal support, advice or an introduction. Usually not another institution's approval. | Sustaining artists and preserving the quality of local cultural life. | Another grand proposal that consumes relationships and does not deliver. | A respectful question, a small completed example and a specific later request. |
| SW12. Experienced collector, adviser or designer | A buying/sourcing brief and possibly acquisition authority. | Suitable work, knowledgeable selection, privacy and reliable delivery. | Generic recommendations, conflicts or unverified stock. | An excellent short selection and transparent seller/fee information. |
| SW13. First buyer, resident or art-minded visitor | Their own preferences, budget, consent and purchase. | A welcoming path to a work they can afford and understand. | Feeling judged, pressured or embarrassed about budget. | Clear choices, prices, no-pressure explanation and a simple next step. |
| SW14. Lodging owner, general manager or marketing lead | A guest-service test, staff allocation and a budget. | A useful reason to choose or recommend the stay, with measurable guest benefit. | Paying for unproved reach or a service staff must deliver themselves. | A defined cohort, funded operator, guest example and limited reporting. |
| SW15. Concierge or front-desk team | Practical guest introductions within the property's policy. | A reliable answer to “where should we see art?” | More training, inaccurate hours and guests returning disappointed. | A small accurate selection and an accountable human contact. |
| SW16. Restaurant owner or manager | Dining/service fit, provider confirmation and any commercial arrangement. | Relevant dining inquiries or a suitable privately scoped event. | Vague exposure, unreliable bookings or disruption during service. | A clear guest request, realistic timing and an existing booking route. |
| SW17. Workshop or experience provider | Capacity, safety, price and provider-specific arrangements. | Suitable participants who understand the offering. | Invented availability, unsuitable groups or unpaid customization. | Approved facts, a real confirmation process and limited initial volume. |
| SW18. Foundation, patron or underwriter | Support within actual charitable/commercial purposes and authority. | Measurable artist/public benefit and responsible operations. | Confused entities, inflated forecasts or paying for private benefit without a clear rationale. | A defined funded activity, honest outcomes and an appropriate applicant/recipient. |
| SW19. Regional venue or local co-producer | Whether a particular city/site could host a show and on what terms. | An artist-centered program that fits local demand and the site. | A traveling format that ignores local relationships and production cost. | A local listening partner, a manageable footprint and a credible delivery plan. |
| SW20. Regional commercial sponsor or host business | A defined sponsorship/service purchase within its budget. | Relevant clients, useful hospitality and contracted benefits. | Paying for logos and an audience that does not exist. | Specific rights, costs and a realistic way to verify delivery. |
| SW21. Editor, journalist or cultural communicator | Editorial interest and coverage under their own standards. | A factual story about artists, work and an observable local experiment. | Promotional claims, puffery or pressure to endorse. | Named independent sources, cleared images and verified outcomes. |
| SW22. Resident, neighbor, teacher or access advocate | Personal participation, advice or matters within their own role. | Welcome, learning, paid local opportunity and manageable community impact. | A visitor project that makes local life harder or excludes residents. | Specific access choices, paid work where appropriate and a way to raise concerns. |
| SW23. Founding board or executive of a mission-led arts foundation | Program development and institutional collaborations within its governance process. | An approved digital contribution that advances a real public/artist program. | Mission dilution, premature commercial association and demands on artists or a new institution's capacity. | A small scoped project responding to a need the institution identifies. Art as Teacher is a specific case, not an assumed funder. |

## Taos relationships that require particular care

An established participant may be enthusiastic about experimentation, skeptical of a particular offer, busy, or simply enjoying an opening. None of those states proves opposition to change. Long service to local institutions gives someone context worth hearing. It does not make them a gate to be overcome.

The [entity directory](04-Taos-Arts-Entity-Directory-2026-10-25.md) maps organizations to roles. Its “possible appeal” is an inference from published purpose and operating needs. It is not evidence that a named person wants this service. A trustee encountered socially cannot approve a museum partnership on the spot. A working artist cannot grant community-wide cultural permission.

## The evidence ladder

Interest in a conversation, permission for follow-up, agreement to a test, a funded contract and a completed outcome are different stages. Record the stage actually reached. An introduction, a business card, a compliment or an opening-night conversation establishes no partnership.
