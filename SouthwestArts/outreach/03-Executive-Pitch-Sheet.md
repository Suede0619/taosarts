# Southwest Arts: executive pitch sheet by audience

**October 25, 2026. Use only after permission to explain.** These scripts describe proposals. At a social event, start with one sentence and let the person choose whether to continue. The full [persona matrix](02-How-and-When-to-Pitch.md#one-offer-for-each-persona) covers every role.

Each script targets 50–70 spoken words, or a calm 25–35 seconds. Evaluation below is editorial judgment about audience fit and claim discipline. It is not a tested conversion score. No script promises a sale, audience, partner or appointment.

| Audience / personas | Primary product | Thirty-second version | Evaluation and next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gallery owners, staff and dealers. SW01/02/04/06 | A useful buyer brief | “We're exploring a service where someone tells us what kind of art they want, their budget and when they can visit. We help prepare a small selection, then connect them with the right seller. You would keep the relationship and use your existing work links. Could I show you one example and ask whether it would save work or create more?” | Strong problem fit if requests are relevant. Ready for a discovery meeting, not a sales-performance claim. |
| Independent artists, cooperatives and Indigenous artists. SW03/05/09 | An artist-controlled introduction | “The aim is to help someone find work they want to live with and connect them directly with its artist or proper seller. Artists would choose what can be shown and how inquiries arrive. Core artist participation is free, with no direct-artist platform commission. We're starting small. What would make a buyer introduction useful and respectful for you?” | Clear artist benefit. Discuss rights, gallery relationships and funded processing before a test. |
| Museums, educators and cultural representatives. SW07/10 | Accurate visits and interpretation | “We're exploring an art-centered visitor service that gives people useful context before they arrive. We'd want your institution's information accurately credited and its access and image policies respected. We would not present a listing as an endorsement. Could we ask for your view of one example, including anything that should not be included or interpreted by us?” | Appropriate only as a listening request. No commercial pitch substitutes for cultural or institutional permission. |
| Longstanding patrons and possible underwriters. SW11/18 | A bounded artist-benefit test | “We'd like to test a small way of turning interest in Taos art into more useful introductions to local sellers. The first task is listening, then trying a few real buyer briefs with willing participants. We'd measure the work, the useful appointments and any confirmed sales. I'd value your judgment about whether that addresses a real need here.” | Low-pressure and locally accountable. Ask for advice before money or introductions. |
| Collectors, advisers, first buyers and visitors. SW12/13 | A personal art selection | “Tell us what you would like to live with, the space you have and what you want to spend. We'd help prepare a small selection of real work and connect you with its artist or gallery. You can ask questions, arrange a viewing or decide it isn't right. The service should make choosing easier without making the decision for you.” | Clear visitor result. Offer only when actual records and delivery staff are ready. |
| Lodging owners and concierge teams. SW14/15 | Art in your stay | “We're exploring an optional art service for guests before or during a Taos stay. A guest shares their interests, receives a small reviewed selection and can request an introduction to a local seller. Your staff would have one accountable contact. Could we test a small cohort and see whether guests use it and whether it adds or reduces staff work?” | Specific service, not advertising reach. Needs an operator, host permission and a bounded test. |
| Restaurants and experience providers. SW16/17 | Relevant direct referrals | “An art visit often needs practical choices around it. We'd like guests to understand your actual offering and choose it because it fits their plans. Your normal confirmation process stays in place, and we wouldn't invent availability or send unqualified groups. Could we look at one example of the kind of guest request you would welcome?” | Concrete provider control. No booking/commission claim before agreement. |
| Regional venues, arts producers and sponsors. SW08/19/20 | One locally shaped show | “We're exploring a small series of art shows, beginning with one edition that fits its host city. The purpose is paid artist opportunity, useful collector conversations and a welcoming public experience. We'd want local partners to shape the format before choosing dates or scale. Could we discuss what would make one event valuable here and what would make it unwelcome?” | Useful discovery pitch. Participation and sponsorship offers wait for feasibility and funding. |
| Residents, teachers and access advocates. SW22 | Local participation | “We're exploring an art service and possible shows that should help working artists and feel worthwhile to people who live here. Before growing the idea, we'd like to understand what access, cost or visitor pressure would concern you. We can explain the small test, but we'd first like to hear what benefit you think local people should actually receive.” | Listening has to affect the design. Do not present consultation as community approval. |
| Editors and cultural communicators. SW21 | A reportable experiment | “We're developing a small test of better introductions between art buyers and local sellers. There is a design prototype, but no live sales network or proved revenue yet. Once there are permissioned examples and observable outcomes, we'd welcome an independent look at what works and what doesn't. Would that kind of evidence-based local story be relevant to your coverage?” | Honest about stage. A stronger media pitch follows real results and cleared sources. |
| Mission-led foundation founders and executives. SW23 | One approved digital encounter | “We're exploring small digital experiences that help people encounter an artist's thinking and find a real program. For a foundation, we'd start with the public purpose, approved material and what your team actually needs. This could be one artist, one question and one useful next step. Would a short conversation about your priorities be welcome before we propose any particular format?” | Mission first. Appropriate for Art as Teacher only as an invitation to listen, not a funding or endorsement request. |

**A useful opening-night sentence:** “We're exploring a way to help a visitor find art they want to live with, then connect them to the right local seller.”

If asked about AI: “It can help prepare a brief and search approved records. People would verify the work and control the introduction. We would explain any live AI feature before you use it.” The [trust note](05-AI-Trust-and-Environmental-Questions.md) gives the longer answer.
