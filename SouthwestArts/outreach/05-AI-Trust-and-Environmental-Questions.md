# AI, artist trust and environmental questions

**October 25, 2026. Communication and design recommendations.** The current prototype's concierge uses scripted responses. It connects no live model, payments or booking service. A proposed AI-assisted collector desk is not an implemented production system.

## What belongs in the pitch

Lead with a useful human result: a better buyer brief, a suitable selection, an accurate visit and a respectful introduction. AI is relevant when explaining how the work happens, demonstrating a real feature, answering a question or obtaining consent. Do not make a technology promise the main reason someone should trust the project.

Answer direct questions honestly. A visitor should know when they are interacting with AI. Sellers should know whether their material will enter an external system before granting permission. Generated demonstration images must remain visibly distinct from real works and places. A manual route should remain available.

### A short answer

> “We use software, including AI where it helps, to prepare and organize information. People review the facts and selection. Artists and galleries control what they permit us to show. We would not let an assistant invent availability, authenticate work, spend your money or make an agreement for you.”

That is a proposed operating standard. Do not claim all production controls are already in place. Explain what currently exists and demonstrate the controls before making them part of a service promise.

## Artist concerns deserve specific answers

| Concern | A useful response and design requirement |
|---|---|
| “Will you make substitutes for our art?” | The proposed service finds and explains actual authorized work. It should not generate replacement inventory, imitate a participating artist's style or invent an artist attribution. |
| “Will you train on our images?” | Do not make a blanket technical promise. Agree permitted use, choose a provider/configuration with appropriate terms, and verify retention/training controls before processing. No upload or model training is authorized by an ordinary listing permission. |
| “Will the system get the story wrong?” | Use approved sources, show uncertainty and route disputed interpretation to a knowledgeable person. Provide a correction process. |
| “Will it take over our customer relationship?” | Share only the specific information the buyer and seller agree to share. Keep existing representation and seller routes intact. |
| “Will my room photo expose my home?” | Offer a photo-free brief. For a later image feature, define access, metadata removal, retention and deletion before collecting images. Do not claim those controls already exist. |
| “Could an AI act without permission?” | Limit tools and permissions. Preparing a shortlist is different from sending a message, reserving something or paying. Use explicit confirmation and human responsibility for consequential actions. |

## Water, energy and climate

Environmental concern is well founded. AI relies on physical infrastructure, electricity, cooling and hardware. The environmental effect varies by workload, data-center design, electricity supply and location. It cannot be inferred accurately from the fact that a page contains an AI feature.

The [IEA's 2026 energy-and-AI summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) describes rapid growth in data-center electricity demand and the substantially different energy requirements of simple text tasks and more intensive reasoning, image or video uses. These are system-level findings, not a measured footprint for this project.

A [2025 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory research summary](https://datacenters.lbl.gov/publications/water-use-data-center-workloads) identifies server efficiency, electricity generation, cooling, climate and utilization among important determinants of workload water use. It supports treating universal “water per prompt” claims with caution. [UNEP's data-center sustainability discussion](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/technical-highlight/how-make-ai-data-centres-more-sustainable) likewise distinguishes energy demand from location- and cooling-dependent water effects.

These sources justify restraint and better measurement. They do not prove the proposed service is sustainable or quantify its water consumption. Do not claim that digital coordination offsets travel, that a green hosting label eliminates impact, or that every interaction consumes a particular bottle of water.

### A respectful answer to an environmental objection

> “The water and energy concerns are real. We haven't measured a production footprint, and I wouldn't claim this is impact-free. We can keep the service small, avoid unnecessary generation, use ordinary search where that is enough, and require better provider information before scaling. You can also use a human or non-AI route.”

Do not turn a gallery opening into a debate about climate or catastrophic AI risk. Someone may decline AI-related participation. Accept that choice. General fears about future systems do not establish what this particular service can do, and a small service's permission controls do not settle the broader debate.

## Practical choices before production

- Use deterministic filters and ordinary records for tasks they handle well. AI should earn its place by improving a measured task.
- Reuse approved results and avoid repeatedly generating the same explanation. Do not generate new art or video to decorate every request.
- Keep prompts and retrieved records limited to the job. Do not send whole private portfolios, buyer files or correspondence by default.
- Use real licensed photographs of actual work when cleared. Generated room illustrations remain labeled concepts, not evidence of a venue or collection.
- Log service quality, model usage and available provider energy/retention information. Report what is unknown. No unmeasured green marketing claim.
- Maintain human accountability, a non-AI route and a way to correct or stop an output.
