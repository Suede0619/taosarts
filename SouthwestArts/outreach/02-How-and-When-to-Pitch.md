# Southwest Arts: how and when to pitch

**October 25, 2026. Conversation guide for review.** The [buyer-led service](../planning/11-Website-Pivot-Collector-and-Visit-Service.md) is the recommended new proposition. Regional shows are a separate proposed offer. Use the [personas](01-Personas-and-Decision-Roles.md) to identify the person's actual role. The team has sent none of these scripts.

## The opening-night rule

Attend the exhibition as a guest. Greet the person, ask about them and the work, and listen. The fact that someone knows a member of the team does not make them a prospect for the evening.

If the project comes up naturally, offer one sentence and stop: “We're exploring a small service that connects people who want to buy art with the artists and galleries that can help.” If the person asks a real question, answer that question. Before explaining more, ask: “Would you like the short version, or is another time better?”

Someone's ability to decline matters more than completing a prepared speech. Moving away, short answers, scanning the room, returning to a companion or changing the subject are reasons to end the pitch. Politeness alone is not buying interest. Do not diagnose a person's motive from their expression.

### A natural example

> “Good to see you. What have you enjoyed in the show?”
>
> After a real conversation, if they ask what the team is working on: “We're testing whether a more useful art-buying introduction could help. Someone tells us what they're looking for, and we help them connect with a suitable artist or gallery. We're trying to avoid giving galleries another catalog to maintain.”
>
> If they want more: “What makes a visitor inquiry useful to you?”
>
> To leave: “I would enjoy hearing more another time, if you'd like. Let's get back to the show.”

Do not ask an artist or gallery to join during that exchange. Do not open the prototype on a phone unless invited. Do not turn the host's event into a recruitment session.

## Two meanings of “when”

### The social moment

| Situation | Appropriate behavior |
|---|---|
| Someone else's opening, performance or dinner | Social conversation. A short answer only when invited. Ask for a later time if interest develops. |
| Gallery staff serving a customer | Wait or arrange another time. The customer and staff's work take priority. |
| Hotel check-in or restaurant service | Ask who handles business conversations and how to request a time. Do not demonstrate software in the service flow. |
| An explicitly arranged discovery meeting | Ask about the person's current process, then show one relevant example. |
| A hosted presentation with a stated topic | Give the agreed version and leave time for questions. |
| No response after an invited follow-up | If consistent with their preference, one brief reminder after a proposed 7–10 days. Then close the loop. No repeated chasing. |

### The business readiness

1. **Problem discovery:** a clear hypothesis and sample brief are enough to ask someone to criticize the idea. Describe it as exploration.
2. **Seller test:** needs consent, a useful request, rights/record handling, the proper seller route and a named operator. It is not a launch or a paid listing sale.
3. **Buyer service:** needs a person able to deliver the promised response, accurate work records, fee disclosure and a human fallback.
4. **Paid host/service agreement:** needs evidence that the work helps, a written scope, delivery capacity, funding and measurable obligations. No guaranteed sales claim.
5. **Regional show recruitment:** needs local input, a feasible site/format, artist terms and a funded production plan before participation promises. A “series” remains a hypothesis until one edition works.

November 1 remains project day one. It is not a deadline for asking everyone to join. A good sequence is internal role agreement, a few invited problem conversations, a bounded funded test, review, then a larger commercial ask. Dates follow readiness and people's availability.

## Objections worth taking seriously

| What they say | Useful response |
|---|---|
| “We already have a website.” | “Yes. We'd want to use your existing work links and seller process. The thing to test is whether the buyer request adds value.” |
| “I don't want another catalog.” | “That is a design constraint. We could begin with responses to individual briefs. If it creates duplicate admin, we should change or stop it.” |
| “Where are your buyers?” | “We haven't established a buyer network. The first test is whether a small host-led cohort uses the service. We can't sell you reach we don't have.” |
| “How does this make money?” | “We are testing funded service work and possible buyer/host support. Direct-artist platform commission remains zero. We need proof that a payer values the service.” |
| “Why should an established gallery care?” | “Only if it helps with a problem you recognize. What makes an inquiry worth your time?” |
| “This sounds like AI replacing judgment.” | “People would control selection, factual approval and sales. The prototype's assistant is scripted. A live AI feature would need separate review and clear labeling.” |
| “We like things as they are.” | “Understood. We don't need to change your operation. Thank you for hearing the question.” |

## Team conduct before any outreach

Agree who wants to attend, who will speak and what each person has agreed to explain. Use accurate introductions. Do not call someone a business partner, salesperson, curator or technical closer without their agreement. Never turn to a companion with “explain it” unless that handoff was agreed in advance and they want it at that moment.

An acceptable pause is: “Let's arrange a time if you'd like to hear more. We're here to enjoy the show tonight.” A person may choose to stop participating in pitches while continuing to build digital assets or business plans. Shared team membership does not imply unlimited duties.
