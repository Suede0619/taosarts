## 1. The business we want to create

Make Taos an unusually rewarding place to discover and buy art, and make the resulting income meaningful to the people who create it. Build the edited year-round art/destination platform and buying service as the core business. A revitalized festival can support that service through public discovery, relationships and visits, subject to separate authority and funding. Start with Taos. Earn the right to become a broader Southwest platform.

The principal product is **confidence and a useful relationship**: a buyer finds real available art, understands its significance and full cost, meets an informed person, purchases easily, and receives the work safely. The website, itinerary, festival, hotel introduction, and AI assistant are ways to deliver that service.

The mission is artist livelihood. Commercial discipline funds the service. Public programming opens the door to people who are not yet collectors. Budget for artist income and public access together.

## 2. The problem and the market hypothesis

Artists need consistent access to buyers, respectful representation, practical selling support, and timely payment. Buyers need trusted selection, reliable information, transparent prices, and a frictionless path from interest to possession. Visitors need clear introductions, routes and hours for dispersed art venues. Galleries need relevant prospects and a service that respects their existing artist relationships. The festival needs operating capacity and comparable outcome data.

The hypothesis is that a coordinated service can convert more of the region's existing cultural interest into completed purchases, while attracting a deliberately cultivated set of new buyers. Test that claim through buyer and seller research. Neither global art-market value nor all Taos tourism spending is an addressable revenue estimate for this project.

### Initial audiences

| Audience | Need | First offer | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-time buyer, often below $1,500 | Confidence without embarrassment | Clear prices, a welcoming introduction, suitable original work | A satisfied first purchase and willingness to return. |
| Established collector, $5,000–$25,000+ target purchase range | Distinctive work and credible access | Curated preview and a personal viewing | Qualified quote, completed purchase, repeat introduction. |
| Advisor, interior designer, architect | Dependable sourcing and specifications | Verified shortlist, trade coordination, installation support | Repeat paid orders with agreed fees. |
| Hotel or institutional procurement buyer | A coherent collection within a real budget | Paid brief, artist selection, phased delivery | Signed, funded milestones completed on time. |
| Artist or representing gallery | Useful demand without loss of control | Free core artist profile. Verified inventory. Opt-in referrals | Net proceeds, repeat buyers, fair treatment. |
| Resident, family, student | Belonging, discovery, learning, enjoyment | Free primary exhibitions, public programs, funded acquisitions | Meaningful participation and community support. |

Price bands organize service needs. They make no judgment about people or artistic quality. Indigenous, Hispano, historical, modern, and experimental work need informed interpretation on their own terms.

## 3. The integrated offer

### A. Curated year-round guide

An edited entry point to art, artists, galleries, architecture, food, lodging, and selected experiences. Begin with a small set of excellent, verified choices. Publish the selection method and distinguish editorial choice from sponsorship. Core artist participation is free. No one can buy a recommendation disguised as independent judgment.

A Taos anchor prevents a regional brand from becoming a generic travel directory. Include Arroyo Seco through locally supported appointments, a feasible half-day route and accurate transport information. Do not imply that rural destinations are an easy extension of a downtown walk or direct crowds to private homes.

### B. Collector and procurement service

A human concierge gathers interests, budget, dimensions, schedule and delivery needs. The concierge checks a shortlist with sellers, arranges viewings, coordinates quotes and follows the purchase through delivery. Private hospitality is optional. The art itself remains accessible to the public.

Build institutional and hospitality procurement as a repeatable second channel. A few substantial projects can diversify festival-season risk, but a speculative commission is not booked sales revenue. Pay artists for development when a buyer requests substantial custom work.

### C. Fall Arts 2027

Use a provisional late-September/early-October planning window until the festival board confirms dates and sites. PASEO publicly lists **September 17–18, 2027**. Calendar coordination offers a first partnership discussion. The project has no joint program. PASEO explicitly says its own program does not feature art booths or sales. [PASEO call](https://paseoproject.org/call-for-artists).

Proposed structure:

- A broad community exhibition with clear eligibility and no application charge after securing underwriting.
- An additional curated collector program with published criteria, invited appointments, and no purchased selection.
- A small number of high-quality public experiences: artist process demonstrations, school/community acquisitions, paid music where appropriate, and partner-led learning.
- Common inventory, wayfinding, staff training, purchase procedures, and fulfillment across participating sales venues.
- A preview period and post-festival follow-up, because some buyers decide before or after attending.

The proposal would use sponsorship to fund free entry. Current festival rules still need confirmation. The existing festival participation page offers artist-submission sponsorships. [Current participation offer](https://taosfallarts.com/participate/).

### D. Cultural and public value

Create pathways for people to learn to choose art. A donor-funded student/community acquisition council can select real works for willing public institutions. Pay artists and cultural advisors. Offer professional development in pricing, documentation, buyer conversations, photography, and fulfillment. Provide useful schedules, transport, seating, communication and respectful staff service alongside free admission.

## 4. Outcomes and definitions

The **legacy combined illustration** uses **$3 million in completed, attributable art sales** across the year, with a proposed $2 million festival-associated component and $1 million year-round component. That historical planning allocation does not set the budget or event dependency of the current platform-first direction. Low and stretch scenarios are $1 million and $5 million. These are targets to validate, **without validated demand forecasts or promises**.

Track five outcome families:

1. **Art sold:** completed paid art value excluding tax, shipping, donations, tickets, refunded orders, and unfulfilled contract balances.
2. **Artist proceeds:** amounts actually paid or payable to artists, separately from gallery margin and project fees. Also median and distribution.
3. **Operating sustainability:** unrestricted cash, earned income, grant restrictions, fully costed labor, and reserve needs.
4. **Public value:** free participation, resident experience, first buyers, learning, and accessibility.
5. **Trust:** inventory accuracy, response time, delivery, complaints, and payout reliability.

The older model allocates **$1.98 million to artist proceeds before production costs and tax**. Artist profit depends on those costs. The operating model yields $579,000 income and $485,000 expenses before unresolved tax and exceptional costs. None of those figures is a historical result. Details and sensitivity are in the [financial plan](02-Financial-Model-and-Targets.md).

The implied 440 buyers from 800 qualified households is **55% conversion**, an aggressive and unproven assumption. Stop presenting the base target as feasible if early cohorts do not support its inputs. “Qualified” means expressed budget, interest, timeframe, permission to follow up, and a plausible purchase opportunity. Not wealth inferred from an address.

## 5. Business model and editorial independence

For Southwest Art direct-artist website sales, take zero platform commission and fund processing through an agreed platform/sponsor budget. Earn from optional paid commercial profiles/services, eligible completed experience referrals, sponsorship and other properly agreed support. Festival/procurement services have separate unapproved terms. The older illustration is not permission to charge artists. A core artist profile and eligibility for editorial consideration should remain free. Paid commercial services can include photography, event production, verified catalog administration, concierge training, or clearly identified advertising.

The repository retains earlier festival, referral and procurement fee assumptions for comparison with separate agreements. They do not apply to direct-artist Southwest Art website sales. Existing representation agreements govern. The proposed policy prohibits double commissions, undisclosed referral fees, compulsory donations and automatic collector-list transfer.

Editors may select a gallery or restaurant without payment. Editors may exclude a paying business when it fails the selection criteria. The commercial team cannot silently rewrite the curator's choices. Publish sponsor labels and a conflict register.

## 6. Governance and decision rights

The festival nonprofit and a possible separate regional business are distinct entities unless authorized leadership decides otherwise. Festival marks, archives, money, artist contacts, and charitable funds are not automatically assets of the new guide. Any shared services or data require a written agreement and fair allocation of costs and benefits.

Proposed roles. The project has made no appointments:

| Role | Accountability | Decision limit |
|---|---|---|
| Festival board / authorized officer | Festival mission, budget, contracts, grants, risk | Retains its legal authority. Delegates explicitly. |
| Product and business lead | Product, partner proposals, measurement, delivery coordination | No authority over nonprofit funds without delegation. |
| Curatorial and collector advisor | Artistic direction, artist/gallerist relationships | Recuses from selection and financial terms involving any affiliated gallery or other commercial interest. |
| Festival producer | Venues, staffing, vendors, operating readiness | Within approved budget and contracts. |
| Collector lead / concierge | Buyer service, appointments, sales follow-up | Cannot promise discounts or authenticity beyond seller approval. |
| Finance / bookkeeping lead | Settlement, tax coordination, reporting, cash forecast | Dual approval for material payments. Artist funds protected. |
| Paid artist/community advisory group | Artist experience, cultural specificity, access | Escalates concerns. Participates in published selection procedures. |

Use three meetings: a 30-minute weekly operating review, a monthly finance/risk review, and a quarterly artist/community feedback session. Keep decisions, conflicts, and changes in a dated log. Appoint a complaints contact independent of a disputed sale's seller where feasible.

## 7. Build sequence and launch conditions

Begin with a small permissioned inventory and a human service process. The user authorized a design prototype and live preview in a separate repository. Use clearly labeled demonstration records and licensed/original images. Review the prototype before production. The production v1 includes contextual AI and ordinary filters as specified in the newer website package. Prototype publication is not permission for live transactions.

The first 90 days should produce a reconstructed 2026 baseline, a signed pilot scope, a few real transactions, quotes for the expensive operating items, a funding pipeline, and an honest go/no-go review. Do not commission a large print run, venue build, or regional advertising campaign before the catalog and response service work.

A festival expansion requires confirmed dates and authority, contracted sites, appropriate insurance and safety plans, a funded operating budget, protected artist payouts, a trained team, and adequate available work. Public programs require their own funding and responsible producers. Reduce scope if organizer funds cannot cover operations. Protect artist balances.

## 8. Risks that can invalidate the plan

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers do not convert at assumed rates | Meetings without quotes or deposits | Interview lost buyers, change inventory/service, lower sales case. |
| Too little suitable work at higher price points | Sellers cannot fulfill buyer shortlists | Recruit appropriate inventory. Do not inflate valuations. |
| Sponsor/grant money arrives late or never | Unfunded commitments in the cash forecast | Stage contracts, obtain actual cash, reduce scope. |
| Reliance on one curator or two unpaid founders | Slow replies, missed tasks | Narrow scope, pay core roles, document handoffs and backups. |
| Cultural or editorial trust is lost | Complaints, undisclosed paid selection | Independent review, corrections, recusal, paid cultural guidance. |
| Artists sell but are not paid promptly | Unreconciled settlements | Separate payout ledger, stop expansion, resolve immediately. |
| Travel, weather, fire, or site failure | Bookings or readiness deteriorate | Remote viewings, alternate indoor program, cancellation plan. |
| Public enjoyment crowds out buying | High attendance, low qualified inquiries | Separate quiet viewings, clear buying service, better matching. |
| Commercial/nonprofit relationship is improper | Unclear ownership and money flows | Written agreement and legal/accounting review before transfers. |

## 9. What success looks like after one year

After one year, assess completed sales, artist payments, returning buyers, public participation and cash available for team pay. Keep records that show each result. Expand only when the pilot supports its costs and obligations.

If the base case proves attainable, reinvest surplus under the correct entity's authority in artist access, staff continuity, reserves, and buyer development. Housing and studio affordability may become a longer-term partnership agenda. This year's plan does not claim that a festival alone can solve them.
