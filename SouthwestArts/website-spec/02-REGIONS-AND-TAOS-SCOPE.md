# Southwest Art regions: a destination taxonomy for later

**Proposal for review, October 19, 2026.** These product regions organize art and travel content. They define no official boundary or tribal territory. Their inclusion does not settle competing definitions of the Southwest. Their purpose is to help a traveler or buyer understand a distinct network of artists and galleries and a realistic visit.

## Recommended structure

Use three levels: **Southwest then destination region then place/neighborhood**. Give artists, works, businesses and experiences a canonical primary region and optional relationships to other regions. Keep a seller's location, artist's home/studio, artwork origin and shipping origin as separate fields. They are not interchangeable.

State lines are useful filters but weak primary navigation for art travel. Large states contain several distinct art markets and travel patterns. A list of every town would make the brand feel like a directory. Start with a manageable destination region, then expand only where we can curate, verify and serve buyers.

The editorial proposal uses these clusters. Before launch in a region, validate its actual artists, community preferences, gallery density, transport patterns, visitor use and commercial relationships. The table does not claim an objective ranking or an existing partner network.

## Four Corners and sovereign communities

Use **Four Corners** as a discovery collection. Identify specific communities and respect tribal authority within it. Navajo Nation/Diné communities, Taos Pueblo, other Pueblos, Ute communities, Hopi and other sovereign communities decide whether and how they participate. Do not group their identities into a decorative style or infer territory from the tourism map.

Link a place to multiple regions using one inventory record. Public material does not authorize visits, photography, use of cultural names, sacred-site mapping, or access to private studios. Geography records should distinguish a public entrance from a general place marker and exclude restricted locations.

## Taos v1 boundary

**Core places:** Taos town and Plaza, Kit Carson and Ledoux areas, Ranchos de Taos, El Prado, Arroyo Seco, Arroyo Hondo and Taos Ski Valley. Use these labels on cards and filters. Public Taos Pueblo visits appear only under the Pueblo's own current rules and consented editorial treatment.

**Selected extended experiences:** Dixon/Embudo/Peñasco, Pilar/Río Grande river activities, Questa/Red River, and Ojo Caliente as clearly identified day trips when they fit the curated offer. They are not all a short walk or equivalent travel time. The High Road has cross-region tags and clear one-way travel assumptions.

**Not silently included:** Santa Fe as a Taos neighborhood, unconsented private studios/homes, closed trails, sacred areas, backcountry access inferred from a map, or a hotel with a matching name in another state/country.

## Navigation rules

- Default to Taos in v1. Display **Taos region** clearly.
- Use a short place filter and optional map. Add region cards as content becomes available.
- A future-region interest form may collect consented interest. It does not imply a launch date.
- Work search can span future regions only after real inventory exists. The buyer may select shipping destination independently.
- Display travel estimates from the chosen origin: Taos Plaza or Arroyo Seco initially, with permissioned device location optional.
- Preserve primary IDs for works, artists, galleries and locations. Cross-region references link to the same product record.

## Region launch test

Expansion needs a paid knowledgeable editor, cleared work, artist relationships, buyer service, delivery capacity, useful experiences, paying partners and local feedback. Confirm capacity to maintain the existing region first. Brand breadth follows service depth.
