# Southwest Art regions — a destination taxonomy for later

**Proposal for review, October 19, 2026.** These are editorial/product regions, not official geographic boundaries, tribal territories, or an assertion that every place below is universally considered part of the Southwest. Their purpose is to help a traveler or buyer understand a distinct art ecosystem and a realistic visit.

## Recommended structure

Use three levels: **Southwest → destination region → place/neighborhood**. Give artists, works, businesses and experiences a canonical primary region and optional relationships to other regions. Keep a seller's location, artist's home/studio, artwork origin and shipping origin as separate fields. They are not interchangeable.

State lines are useful filters but weak primary navigation for art travel. Large states contain several distinct art markets and travel patterns. A list of every town would make the brand feel like a directory. Start with a manageable destination region, then expand only where we can curate, verify and serve buyers.

## Four Corners and sovereign communities

Use **Four Corners** initially as a cross-region discovery collection, not a replacement for specific communities or tribal authority. Navajo Nation/Diné communities, Taos Pueblo, other Pueblos, Ute communities, Hopi and other sovereign communities decide whether and how they participate. Do not group their identities into a decorative style or infer territory from the tourism map.

A place can link to multiple destination regions without being duplicated as separate inventory. Public material does not authorize visits, photography, use of cultural names, sacred-site mapping, or access to private studios. Geography records should distinguish a public entrance from a general place marker and exclude restricted locations.

## Taos v1 boundary

**Core:** Taos town/Plaza, Kit Carson and Ledoux areas; Ranchos de Taos; El Prado; Arroyo Seco; Arroyo Hondo; Taos Ski Valley. Use these labels on cards and filters. Public Taos Pueblo visits appear only under the Pueblo's own current rules and consented editorial treatment.

**Selected extended experiences:** Dixon/Embudo/Peñasco, Pilar/Río Grande river activities, Questa/Red River, and Ojo Caliente as clearly identified day trips when they fit the curated offer. They are not all a short walk or equivalent travel time. The High Road has cross-region tags and clear one-way travel assumptions.

**Not silently included:** Santa Fe as a Taos neighborhood, unconsented private studios/homes, closed trails, sacred areas, backcountry access inferred from a map, or a hotel with a matching name in another state/country.

## Navigation rules

- Default to Taos in v1; display **Taos region** clearly.
- Use a short place filter and optional map rather than thirteen empty region cards.
- A future-region interest form may collect consented interest; it does not imply a launch date.
- Work search can span future regions only after real inventory exists. The buyer may select shipping destination independently.
- Display travel estimates from the chosen origin: Taos Plaza or Arroyo Seco initially, with permissioned device location optional.
- Preserve primary IDs for works, artists, galleries and locations. A cross-region reference is a relationship, not a copied product.

## Region launch test

A proposed new region needs a knowledgeable paid/contracted curator or editor, sufficient cleared work and artist relationships, viable buyer service and delivery, a few reliable experiences, partner willingness to pay, cultural/local feedback, and evidence that the existing region can be maintained. Brand breadth follows service depth.
