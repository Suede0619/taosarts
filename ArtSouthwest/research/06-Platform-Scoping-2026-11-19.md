# November 18–19 platform scoping

The November discussions gave ArtSouthwest a concrete itinerary to test and a clearer editorial remit. Visitors could browse art and artists or ask for a personalized visit. A blog could explain artists, movements and local history, while a newsletter could help readers keep discovering work. The three meeting summaries supply planning evidence. The [project baseline](../PROJECT-BASELINE.md) governs geography, artist economics and production review.

## The first itinerary test

Use one fictional, same-day Taos visit to test the flow proposed in the meetings. Offer location permission or an entered starting point. Collect interests, budget and a time window, such as three hours, five hours or a full day. Return a map and a readable list with a small number of stops, estimated travel, opening windows, access notes and the next step for each stop. Test whether the itinerary helps a visitor discover art and reach a relevant seller or venue. Compare a map and list with the proposed magazine treatment only if it helps visitors use the plan.

Use fixture or approved records and label simulated status. Test denied location permission, a closed or stale stop and a route that runs past the time limit. Show the source and time checked for availability. If a provider can confirm only opening hours, label availability as unconfirmed and show how to check. Keep reservations and tickets as a later integration that depends on provider permission, reliable inventory and explicit visitor confirmation. Track route and source errors, review minutes, response time, usage cost per usable itinerary and whether a visitor understands what remains to confirm. See the [AI specification](../website-spec/04-AI-CONCIERGE-AND-PLATFORM.md).

## Editorial responsibility and audience

An editor would own the blog and newsletter, check sources, obtain rights to reproduce material and request review from the relevant cultural authority when needed. Taos’s Indigenous, Hispano and other community histories call for precise attribution and care around imagery, ceremony, access and interpretation. Hold content when its publication authority is unclear. Apply the same review to catalog captions, route descriptions and the material the assistant can draw on. Keep the source, review status and correction route with each published record. An AI draft still needs an accountable human reviewer.

A proposed early audience test pairs Instagram posts using cleared material with an article or newsletter signup. Measure article visits, signups and useful artist or visit inquiries to learn whether the channel reaches the intended audience before relying on paid acquisition or a gate.

## Choices before further build work

- **Product and region.** Test whether visitors understand both entry points on one landing page. Decide later whether the itinerary and catalog need separate identities or distribution. The November 19 summary frames the concept around Santa Fe and discusses Arizona as another regional lead. The project baseline still specifies Taos as the only v1 region.
- **Economics.** Free-at-launch access was a preference in the follow-up. A teaser or usage cap followed by payment, hospitality subscriptions and facilitated-sale fees remain hypotheses. The October pilot also offers host underwriting and optional paid human service as alternatives to test. Zero ArtSouthwest commission on direct-artist website sales remains the baseline. A fee on any other transaction needs a defined channel, payer, service and seller agreement.
- **Cost and operations.** Determine who would own application billing and usage limits. Check the deployed API account and budget separately from any existing assistant subscription. Include routing or provider charges and human review time in the cost of a usable plan. Assign responsibility for catalog onboarding, editorial review, interaction design and ongoing technical support before committing capacity.
- **Scope and timing.** The November 19 notes put written scope and workstream ownership first. The November 18 itinerary discussion proposed an interactive proof of concept using the existing preview as a starting point. The April timing discussion is a working target tied to onboarding and capacity. The summaries record exploratory participation and interest in editorial and regional roles. Appointments, pay, approvals and launch timing remain open.
