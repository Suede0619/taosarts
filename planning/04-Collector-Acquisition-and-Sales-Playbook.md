# Collector acquisition and the art-sales service

Proposed operating playbook, October 19, 2026. Relationships, inventory, and partnerships must be opt-in. No outreach has been sent as part of this document work.

Day one is **November 1, 2026**. Outreach, spending and sales activity require their own actual authority; publication of a demonstration prototype is not live seller onboarding. Website direct-artist sales take zero platform commission. [Current baseline](../PROJECT-BASELINE.md).

## Sell the right work to the right person

Begin with buyer needs rather than a mass mailing. A useful brief records budget, interests, dimensions, medium, intended setting, delivery date, purchase authority, existing collection, and how the person prefers to communicate. Ask what they like and why; avoid performing expertise at them. A first buyer deserves the same clarity as a major collector.

For established collectors, offer knowledgeable access to distinctive work and the artist's thinking. For advisors and designers, offer dependable specifications, representation, lead times, and handling. For hospitality buyers, offer a coherent procurement process with real approvals and funded milestones. For visitors, remove practical friction: how to view, what it costs, and how it gets home.

## Priority channels and capped tests

| Denver feeder market | Two small host-led previews or remote sessions; cap each at a proposed $1,500 incremental spend | Qualified households, cost, quotes, paid purchases over 90 days | Repeat only when contribution or a credible repeat pipeline supports cost. |
| Hospitality concierges | Train five willing desks and provide a tagged appointment link | Referral quality, response time, buyer outcomes | Improve script before adding more hotels. |
| HeyTaos | Licensed editorial and tagged landing-page pilot | Qualified appointments and attributed paid orders | Renew on results, not page impressions. |
| Advisors / designers / architects | Ten tailored introductions around actual available work | Sourcing briefs, specification requests, funded orders | Invest in repeat accounts with positive contribution. |
| Institutions / hospitality procurement | Five discovery conversations with budget holders | Approved briefs, paid design work, delivery milestones | No unpaid speculative production. |
| Earned editorial | A few strong stories about artists, process, and a reason to visit | Useful inquiries and source-attributed appointments | Retain stories that bring relevant people. |
| Paid search / social | Small price-band and appointment tests after catalog accuracy | Cost per qualified appointment and per paid household | Pause if inquiries cannot be served or acquisition cost exceeds margin. |

Initial priority is Taos relationships plus Denver. Santa Fe, Albuquerque, Dallas, Phoenix, and New York are secondary test markets, not a simultaneous launch. Choose markets based on real introductions, travel feasibility, and matched inventory.

## A concierge transaction, from first contact to repeat buyer

1. **Respond within one business day.** Confirm interest, consent, timeline, and a budget range. During the festival, staff an explicit live service window.
2. **Qualify respectfully.** A qualified prospect has a plausible purchase need and permission to follow up. Never infer wealth or identity from private demographic data.
3. **Send three to five suitable works.** Include artist, title, dimensions, medium, date, edition, price, availability timestamp, representation, and a concise reason each fits. Ask permission before widening the search.
4. **Arrange a real viewing.** Confirm location, access, time, host, and travel. Offer a remote option. A private home or studio appears only with the occupant's consent.
5. **Create an all-in quote.** Separate art price, discount, tax, packing, insurance, shipping, installation, and any service fee. State the seller, validity, hold, return/cancellation, and delivery terms.
6. **Confirm availability and hold.** One shared status record prevents multiple sellers promising the same work. A proposed human viewing/quote hold is 48 hours if the seller agrees. The website’s separate checkout hold is a proposed ten minutes. Neither is a fabricated scarcity claim.
7. **Use an authorized payment route.** Hosted payment or verified seller invoice, with fraud checks appropriate to value. Confirm changed bank details through a known channel.
8. **Fulfill and settle.** Condition documentation, packing/insurance, tracking, delivery acceptance, and timely artist/gallery payment. Reconcile the ledger.
9. **Follow up at approximately 48 hours after delivery, 14 days, and 90 days**, with the buyer's communication preferences. Resolve problems first; ask for introductions only after a satisfactory experience.

A person who declines should leave with respect and useful knowledge. Record the reason in a short structured field: wrong work, price, timing, travel, confidence, delivery, or no current intent. Do not pressure staff to convert every conversation.

## Procurement and larger commissions

Use a real brief, decision maker, site specifications, budget, approval dates, accessibility/safety requirements, and payment schedule. Pay substantial concept development separately. Document copyright, reproduction, site preparation, fabrication, installation, ownership, maintenance, and changes. A commission's face value is pipeline; only qualifying delivered paid milestones enter the sales target.

A regional procurement desk can help hotels, clinics, workplaces, and designers buy locally without each reinventing sourcing. It should not demand free speculative work from many artists or disguise an unpaid contest as opportunity.

## Public access and the first-buyer ladder

Make prices visible and welcome questions. Offer a clear way to buy at several price points without asking every artist to produce an inexpensive line. Use original works, legitimate editions, and artist-approved products with honest descriptions. A print is not an original painting; a limited edition needs a real edition record.

Possible pathways include a no-pressure introduction, a short artist conversation, a small purchase, a return visit, and a larger commission over time. Do not sell art as a guaranteed financial investment or use credit pressure to manufacture demand. Any installments should use an approved provider and transparent terms, not an improvised lending scheme.

## Event buyer experience

Before arrival, send a verified preview and ask for preferences. Make accommodation and transport information factual, with partner benefits disclosed. At the event, offer quiet appointments, a staffed purchase/help desk, and visible prices. Let public discovery and collector service coexist; do not reserve all compelling art or cultural programming behind a hospitality paywall.

After the event, hold a defined follow-up period for quotes and remote viewings. Attribute a later purchase only with a recorded link and agreed time window. Report festival-period sales and post-event completions separately when comparing years.

## Sales management

Use stages: **new → contacted → qualified → shortlist → viewing → quote → hold/deposit → paid → delivered → settled**, with lost/cancelled/refunded alternatives. Each active opportunity needs an owner, next action, and date. Count households consistently; partners at one household are not automatically two buyers.

Review stalled opportunities weekly. Assess channel contribution after referral fees, processing, paid media, travel, and allocated service labor. Track artist distribution alongside sales concentration. A few large purchases can create a good headline and a fragile business.

Commercial claims must be factual. Do not promise museum acquisitions, tax benefits, appreciation, prestigious provenance, partner endorsement, or a sold-out event without supporting evidence. Obtain the artist's permission for images, biographies, and stories; do not invent personal meaning to make a sale.
