# Festival operations, artist equity, and governance

Proposed requirements for the next edition, prepared October 19, 2026. Authorized festival leadership must confirm dates, sites, permissions, budgets and professional sign-offs.

Project year: **November 1, 2026–October 31, 2027**. Direct-artist Southwest Art website sales have zero platform commission and separate funded processing terms. Festival agreements remain distinct. [Current baseline](../PROJECT-BASELINE.md).

## Operating principles

An event that attracts people but cannot sell, protect, deliver, and settle the art has not fulfilled its mission. Every participating site needs a named responsible person, defined opening hours, a purchase route, and a common incident/escalation process. Every sold work needs a seller, a valid agreement, a condition record, and a payout obligation.

The festival should preserve broad artist participation while making buyer discovery more useful. A community exhibition and an additional curated program can coexist if criteria, fees, space limits, jury composition, conflicts, and appeals/corrections are public. Editors select the curated program independently of payment.

## Artist offer and representation

Proposed offer: free core artist listing and application, sponsor-underwritten participation where feasible, practical training, accurate representation, fair contracts, and timely payment. Fund the subsidy before promising it. Existing festival rules are not assumed to match this proposal.

The artist agreement should address:

- Legal seller and artist identity, eligibility, representation, and authority to consign.
- Specific works, agreed prices, discount permissions, editions, and image rights.
- Commission or referral terms, all deductions, tax, and who may approve a change.
- Consignment dates, title, custody, loss, insurance, handling, and transport.
- Holds, availability updates, returns, cancellations, chargebacks, and unsold-work collection.
- Settlement timing, itemized statements, dispute handling, and contact details.
- Copyright retained by the artist unless separately agreed. Promotional license limited by medium, term, purpose, and takedown rights.
- No obligation to donate work, buy advertising, disclose collector lists, or accept unpaid custom design work.

Respect existing gallery representation. Respect representation agreements and disclose every commission. Record the agreed seller and fee allocation before displaying the work as available.

## Selection, cultural authority, and conflicts

Pay a small artist/community advisory group, including people invited through appropriate Pueblo and Hispano relationships. Participation is voluntary. Representation is not achieved by adding a single person to approve all cultural matters. Communities choose what to share and how to receive credit.

Publish eligibility and selection criteria before applications. Disclose jurors, manage conflicts, offer accessible submission help, and provide a process for factual corrections and concerns. Every decision maker must recuse from selection, fee and preferential-placement decisions involving an affiliated gallery or other commercial interest. Apply the same disclosed conflict rules to all participants.

Native authorship and tribal affiliation must come from appropriate artist documentation and consent, never AI inference. Marketing must be accurate under the [Indian Arts and Crafts Act](https://www.doi.gov/node/41066). Online visibility grants no permission to use sacred places, ceremonies, private studios, symbols or restricted knowledge. Obtain specific permissions for photography, storytelling, and visits.

## Nonprofit and commercial structure

Before sharing money or assets, identify which entity owns each service, takes payments, signs contracts, controls marks, and bears liability. A separate commercial guide needs a written arm's-length agreement for any use of festival resources. Document service value, cost allocation, conflicts, access to data, termination, and archive ownership. Charitable funds must support the nonprofit's purposes. They are not automatically startup capital for an affiliated business. [IRS: private benefit and inurement](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/inurement-private-benefit-charitable-organizations).

Separate donor recognition from commercial advertising. Sponsorship benefits that include promotion or substantial return benefits can receive different tax treatment from simple acknowledgment. Have accounting review the actual package. Do not promise deductibility. [IRS sponsorship guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/advertising-or-qualified-sponsorship-payments).

## Sales, tax, and settlement

Assign merchant/seller responsibility by channel. Confirm registration, invoicing, exemptions if applicable, marketplace obligations, and consignment treatment with a New Mexico accountant before transactions. Nonprofit status does not answer every tax question. New Mexico describes GRT as a business tax and requires separate invoice statement when passed to the purchaser. [GRT overview](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/businesses/gross-receipts-overview/), [nonprofit guidance](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/businesses/information-for-non-profits/).

The state has published a consignment case in which an artist's assumption that the gallery handled tax did not resolve the artist's own obligations. Assign each filing to a responsible person and verify submission. [Taxation and Revenue: Dennis Champlin](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/all-nm-taxes/2022/11/28/dennis-champlin/). This plan does not determine the tax treatment of a particular arrangement.

Use a protected settlement ledger and appropriate account controls. Reconcile daily during the event. Require two-person approval for material transfers and confirm beneficiary changes through a known contact. Provide an itemized artist statement. Target payment within seven business days after cleared funds and contractual acceptance, subject to the agreed return/chargeback terms. Report every exception. Unpaid artist balances, tax, refunds, and shipping obligations are not cash available for operations.

## Venue and production checklist

| Workstream | Required result | Responsible role |
|---|---|---|
| Site rights and capacity | Signed permission, hours, floor plan, occupancy, access, load-in/out | Producer + site owner |
| Insurance and custody | Appropriate coverage, deductibles, exclusions, valuations, certificates, incident procedure | Producer + broker + seller |
| Artwork handling | Intake ID, photos/condition, secure storage, approved hanging and lighting, trained handlers | Registrar / handling lead |
| Security | Opening/closing controls, staffing, overnight arrangements, cash/device security, emergency contacts | Security lead |
| Accessibility | Step-free access where possible, disclosed limitations, seating, readable labels, service information, contact for needs | Access lead |
| Fire and public safety | Applicable approvals, egress, capacity management, first aid, emergency coordination | Producer + responsible authorities |
| Music / food / alcohol | Paid performer agreements, appropriate permissions/licenses and responsible vendors | Program producer |
| Facilities | Toilets, water, waste, weather shelter, cleaning, power, connectivity | Site manager |
| Transport and navigation | Accurate map, parking/access information, quoted shuttle capacity if used | Visitor services |
| Sales desk | Trained staff, live inventory, reliable connectivity and backup, clear seller invoices | Sales operations |
| Fulfillment | Packing/insurance quotes, pickup rules, delivery status, condition on handover | Fulfillment lead |
| Public communication | Confirmed schedule, correction owner, cancellation channels, clear access information | Communications lead |

Do not solve a budget gap by omitting essential safety, access, or artwork protection. Reduce program scope instead. Confirm local permissions and costs for the actual venue and format.

## Proposed program architecture and day of operation

Set the final program after confirming dates. Choose a few funded public programs the team can deliver well. Coordinate gallery and evening hours so visitors have time to explore.

| Time relative to public opening | Work |
|---|---|
| 8–12 weeks before | Lock rights, seller terms, insurance plan, critical vendors, public program and access budget. |
| 4 weeks before | Audit work and images. Verify prices. Train staff. Test sales and outage procedures. |
| Load-in | Scan/check work, photograph condition, verify labels, document custody. |
| Daily before opening | Safety walkthrough, availability audit, staff briefing, appointment review. |
| Public hours | Welcoming hosts, quiet art conversations, staffed purchase desk, accurate holds and access support. |
| Scheduled programs | Short artist/process sessions and separately produced music/public experiences. |
| Daily close | Secure art, reconcile orders and funds, log incidents, update next day's availability. |
| Load-out | Release only to authorized people against records. Capture condition and custody. |
| First week after | Complete delivery and settlements. Resolve exceptions. Follow up with buyers. |
| By Oct 31, 2027 where feasible | Publish provisional outcome report, clearly separating unsettled balances and later completions. |

If the confirmed festival ends close to or after October 31, extend the closeout schedule and distinguish the planning year's result from the edition's final result. Do not force transactions into the wrong period to meet the target.

## Contingencies and escalation

| Trigger | Immediate action | Decision maker |
|---|---|---|
| Severe weather, smoke/fire, or unsafe site | Follow responsible authority guidance. Close/relocate only to approved alternatives. Notify public and buyers | Producer / safety authority |
| Payment or internet outage | Preserve inquiry/hold records securely. Use approved backup invoice process. No unsecured card collection | Sales operations |
| Work damaged or missing | Protect people and site, document condition/custody, contact insurer and owner, suspend sale | Registrar + producer |
| Oversold or unavailable work | Stop payment/confirm refund under contract, contact buyer honestly, correct inventory | Seller + concierge |
| Artist payment discrepancy | Freeze disputed transfer, reconcile evidence, escalate promptly | Finance + independent reviewer |
| Funding shortfall | Halt optional commitments. Protect liabilities. Activate reduced program | Authorized budget owner |
| Cultural or editorial complaint | Acknowledge, pause contested material where appropriate, seek relevant authority, document correction | Community lead + independent reviewer |

A remote mesa gathering is outside year-one scope. It would require a separate business case, landowner and cultural consent, permits, emergency services, sanitation, water, fire planning, transport, insurance, and funding. A minimal-build aesthetic does not remove those costs.

## Artist participation and income measures

Report total and median artist proceeds, share of participating artists with a sale, first-time sellers, time to payment, top-ten share of sales, and voluntary demographic/community participation where appropriate and consented. Use small-group suppression and do not require disclosure of identity to receive fair treatment.

Count free and paid program attendance separately. Distinguish visits from unique people. Sample learning and resident experience without making every public visitor register. Record access complaints and improvements. Long-term studio/housing work needs specialist partners and a separate plan. The festival should not claim it has solved affordability because aggregate sales rose.
