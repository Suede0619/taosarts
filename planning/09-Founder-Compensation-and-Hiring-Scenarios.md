# Founder compensation, profit sharing and hiring — three scenarios

**Brainstorm and decision brief · October 21, 2026 · USD.** Project day one is **November 1, 2026**; year one ends October 31, 2027. Every amount, fee, ownership percentage and hiring threshold below is a **proposal for discussion**, not agreed pay, a market-rate finding, funding already secured or a forecast.

The aim is to pay for the work of building a useful art business while helping artists remain in Taos. Founder pay belongs in the economics from the beginning. Its start date must follow actual funding and delivery capacity.

This is a **standalone commercial website/service illustration**, separate from the legacy festival model. It does not change [that model's inputs or outputs](02-Financial-Model-and-Targets.md), add its assumed commissions, authorize festival expenditure or establish a consolidated budget. The [website commerce proposal](../website-spec/05-COMMERCE-PRICING-AND-OPERATIONS.md) still controls free core artist inclusion, zero direct-artist platform commission and the proposed profile fees. Commercial production still requires prototype review and operating readiness.

## 1. The recommendation

**Start by testing Scenario A. Move to B only with real underwriting, or toward C with repeat paying clients. Hire to remove a measured delivery bottleneck after funding the role.** The scenarios are alternatives, not three income streams to add together or automatic stages on a calendar.

The website can introduce buyers, but an early business also needs something a commercial client will pay for before a large audience exists: excellent object documentation, a defined hospitality art brief, a curated sourcing service or another separately scoped service. These must support the art mission and respect existing sellers. A general custom-website agency would be a different business.

If the founders need more personal income than the selected scenario provides, maintain outside income, reduce project hours or secure more funding. Equity and deferred promises do not fund current living expenses. Actual personal income requirements, available hours and maximum cash exposure should be recorded privately before choosing a scenario.

## 2. Separate five kinds of money

| Kind | What it means | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Artist/gallery money | Art subtotal owed to its actual seller, plus separately handled tax, shipping and refunds | Never founder income or operating runway. Zero direct-artist platform commission remains in force. |
| Pay for work | Defined product, business, curatorial, collector-service and operational duties | A funded, written monthly scope and compensation arrangement; reviewed when hours or duties change. |
| Ownership | Long-term rights, voting and economic participation in the commercial entity | Negotiate separately from monthly pay. No split is established by this document. |
| Founder financing | Cash invested or lent to the company | Record as capital or a documented loan, not revenue. State repayment, risk and priority explicitly. |
| Distributable cash | Cash actually available after delivery obligations, costs, tax provision, debt commitments and reserve requirements | Allocate only under the agreed ownership and distribution policy. Accounting profit alone is insufficient. |

Do not split gross art sales or gross business receipts between founders. A large amount flowing through an account may belong to artists, clients, tax authorities or suppliers.

### Two complementary founder roles

| Role | Core responsibility | Evidence of useful work |
|---|---|---|
| Product and business founder | Product/design, operating system, commercial proposals, pricing, measurement and delivery coordination | Working discovery/service flows, paid client work, accurate reporting and manageable costs. |
| Curatorial and collector founder | Selection, artist/gallery relationships, object context, collector conversations and service quality | Suitable cleared inventory, useful viewings, completed service briefs and satisfied buyers/sellers. |

Both roles create value. Contacts alone are not booked income, and code alone is not a functioning selling service. Equal pay is used in the examples for simplicity; actual hours, responsibility and commitments may justify different pay without automatically changing ownership.

## 6. Scenario A — Lean paid pilot

**Best fit:** limited starting cash, part-time founder availability and willingness to sell/deliver a few concrete services before expanding software or staffing.

**Pay mechanism:** $1,500/month for each founder against a bounded service scope, initially roughly 40–50 hours per founder per month. This is a part-time example, not full-time compensation. Record actual hours and scope; if delivery routinely exceeds the budget, change pricing or reduce commitments.

**Why it can work:** fourteen paying profiles contribute $2,092; two delivered $2,500 packages add $5,000. External project costs are funded in the budget. The $1,192 monthly cushion supports tax/reserve needs before any distribution. Fourteen live paying profiles and two packages every month are still a meaningful sales task; none should be assumed from a launch announcement.

**Before the first recurring pay commitment:** signed deliverable scopes, actual collected funds available for earned work, a costed calendar and the $17,700 conservative buffer. Founder financing can establish the buffer, but it is not revenue and must have written terms. One-time setup costs are extra. If this cannot be funded, authorize only a smaller paid milestone whose costs are covered; do not promise recurring pay or quietly accrue unlimited back pay.

**Ownership/profit idea:** an illustrative 50/50 partnership if long-term contribution, risk and commitment are genuinely comparable. Retain all residual cash during the first 90 days. Once obligations and the reserve floor are satisfied, a possible quarterly policy is **50% retained, 50% distributed to owners**: 25% of eligible cash to each equal owner. This is additional to funded service pay, not a share of art proceeds.

**People:** use a bookkeeper and genuinely independent specialists only for defined, funded work. Their costs must fit the existing overhead/project budgets. No continuing employee is funded here. A $1,500/month additional role would change the $1,192 surplus to a $308 deficit if no contribution is added.

**Failure test:** one project rather than two cuts revenue by $2,500 and saves approximately $500 external delivery cost, leaving an **$808 monthly deficit**. Do not resign from outside income on the strength of two untested sales assumptions.

## 7. Scenario B — Underwritten operation

**Best fit:** a commercial supporter values a well-defined regional art service enough to fund six months while customer revenue develops.

**Pay mechanism:** $4,000/month gross for each founder, with written duties and an initial capacity assumption of roughly 100–120 hours per founder per month. Verify actual needs and workload; expand scope only with an affordable revised budget. The monthly company budget includes $2,000 additional founder compensation costs and $1,500 of part-time operations/content capacity. Do not call the gross figure take-home pay or automatically treat every payment as a salary.

**Why it can work:** $10,000 underwriting + $4,184 profiles + $800 completed referrals + $2,500 earned project work = $17,484, covering $14,400 costs and leaving $3,084 before income tax, exceptional costs and distributions.

**Funding condition:** the six-month $60,000 commercial underwriting contract must be real and collectible, with permissions, deliverables, reporting, payment dates and cancellation/refund terms. The separate $43,200 conservative cash buffer must be identified after other obligations; a pledge is not cash. A prepaid underwriting agreement is earned over delivery and must not be counted twice as monthly revenue and additional free profit/reserve. A finance reviewer must reconcile any prepaid funding to remaining delivery obligations before counting available runway.

**Ownership/profit idea:** ownership is negotiated independently; a supporter buying a service gets no automatic founder equity. An equal-founder model is one option, not a grant of rights. **Retain residual cash during the six-month underwriting term** to build renewal/replacement capacity; do not fund a founder bonus from refundable or restricted money. Revisit distributions only after the renewal/replacement plan and reserve test pass.

**People:** add the $1,500/month part-time operations role only as a bounded, fully funded assignment with an appropriate employment/contract structure. It handles cleared content, record follow-up, appointment administration and reporting, freeing founder time for curation, sales and product. Include its entire six-month $9,000 cost and any exit obligations in the funded scope. This is an exception to waiting for three months of customer revenue only when the whole term is genuinely funded; it is not permission for an unfunded permanent hire.

**Failure test:** without underwriting, the same operation loses **$6,916/month**. Start renewal or replacement planning at least 90 days before the term ends. Without a credible replacement, stop new discretionary commitments and move toward the smaller scope before funds run out. Employment and contractual obligations still have to be met.

## 8. Scenario C — Service-led growth with a small team

**Best fit:** repeated paid commercial briefs, a reliable delivery process and measurable customer demand beyond listing fees.

**Pay mechanism:** $6,000/month gross per founder plus a $3,000 combined compensation-cost allowance, initially budgeting roughly 140–160 hours per founder per month. Add one operations/client-service role at **$6,000/month fully loaded**, including its employment costs and any agreed benefits. External specialists remain attached to costed projects/retainers. These are budget assumptions, not verified recruiting quotes.

**Why it can work:** five separately scoped $2,000 retainers, two completed $12,500 service milestones, the full profile cohort and eligible referrals produce $40,784/month. Costs of $31,500 include founder pay, the operations role, direct project/retainer delivery and the artist-processing subsidy. The $9,284 cushion remains before income tax, a team bonus or owner distributions.

**Demand and capacity condition:** two $12,500 milestones every month mean 24 such milestones at a full-year pace. That is a substantial professional-service business, not an automatic result of publishing a beautiful website. Test one paid scope first, record founder/staff/specialist hours, cap revisions and measure margin. Five recurring clients must have actual monthly work and renewal value. Assign founder hours for sales, curation and product as well as delivery. Reduce volume assumptions if the service cannot fit available capacity.

**Ownership/profit idea:** retain the founders' negotiated ownership; hiring does not automatically give someone equity. A possible quarterly residual-cash policy is **50% retained, 10% to a team incentive pool at total company cost, and 40% to owners**. With equal ownership, that is 20% of eligible cash to each founder. Any equity/profit-interest grant is a separate, professionally drafted decision; a cash bonus is not an ownership grant.

**When the operations hire is affordable:** all hiring gates in section 11 must pass. The five retainers generate an illustrative $9,000 contribution after $1,000 external retainer costs: **1.5 times** the $6,000 role cost, before other company overhead and founder labor already budgeted. The entire company must still cover its costs; that single ratio is insufficient. Require the $94,500 conservative cash buffer and a credible funded next-quarter delivery plan. Use paid, bounded support while demand is being proved rather than treating future leads as payroll funding.

**Failure test:** one project rather than two reduces revenue by $12,500 and saves approximately $2,000 direct cost, leaving a **$1,216 monthly deficit**. Losing both project milestones produces an **$11,716 monthly deficit** even with the five retainers. Stagger clients/milestones, monitor concentration and preserve cash; project dependence is not cured by calling the business recurring.

## 10. How ownership and profit sharing could be agreed

### A starting proposal, not a predetermined split

Consider **50/50 ownership** only if both founders commit comparable long-term effort, risk and responsibility. The two roles can have different monthly pay if duties or hours differ. If commitment is materially unequal, compare a documented 60/40 allocation, milestone-earned ownership or a paid advisory arrangement with limited earned equity. Do not assign a larger share merely because one contribution is easier to count than another.

A discussion example is founder ownership earned over 36 months with a six-month initial commitment test, credit for explicitly documented prior contributions and defined treatment of departure, illness and termination. This is a proposed negotiating device, not a claim about standard market terms or an enforceable vesting agreement. Legal/tax review must address the actual entity, grant dates, repurchase terms and any time-sensitive elections before interests are issued.

Record cash advances separately: loan or capital, repayment terms, whether repayment precedes distributions and who can approve new debt. Do not allocate the same money both priority loan repayment and additional ownership without an explicit agreement. Record unpaid founder work as a chosen contribution or an actual approved obligation; avoid an unexplained running claim for back pay.

### Cash allocation example

Suppose a quarter has **$10,000 genuinely eligible residual cash** after all costs, tax provision, debt commitments, service obligations and the reserve floor. This is a separate round-number example, not one of the monthly pre-tax surpluses above.

| Proposed policy | Retained in business | Team incentive, total company cost | Owner distributions | Each equal founder |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A, after the initial hold and reserve test | $5,000 | $0 | $5,000 | $2,500 |
| B, during underwriting term | $10,000 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| C, once all gates pass | $5,000 | $1,000 | $4,000 | $2,000 |

The team pool includes any employer costs; its gross recipient bonuses may be lower. A finance reviewer must determine actual tax/accounting treatment and avoid double-counting a bonus cost. Pass-through owners may owe tax even when cash is retained; tax allocations and any permitted tax distributions need their own written treatment before applying the discretionary split. Founder pay for work is already an operating commitment, not something deducted a second time here.

### Decisions that belong in the founder agreement

Roles and hours; initial pay and review triggers; ownership and earned interests; contributed cash and prior work; IP ownership/licensing; existing gallery/client conflicts; authority for contracts and bank payments; expense approval; tax distributions; discretionary profit policy; hiring authority; information rights; dispute/deadlock resolution; departure, incapacity and buyout process. Equal ownership needs an explicit deadlock route. No founder can unilaterally approve their own increase or an undisclosed payment to an affiliated business.

## 11. When to bring on new people

Hire for work the business already needs and can fund. “We expect more traffic” or “we sold a lot of art” is not an operating-income test.

| Capacity to add | When and why | Funding gate |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeping/accounting support | Before real money starts moving; clear books, tax setup and separation of seller/client funds | Written scope within overhead or a separately approved startup budget; do not postpone essential controls to afford discretionary founder distributions. |
| Photographer, specialist writer, rights/metadata help, occasional technical specialist | A signed project needs a defined deliverable outside current capacity | Approved cost and deliverables funded within that project's cash plan; no open-ended retainer disguised as a small task. |
| Part-time content/operations support | Repeated record follow-up, publishing or appointment administration is displacing useful founder work for several weeks | B's fully funded term, or at least $2,250/month incremental net contribution for a $1,500 fully loaded role, alongside whole-business cash coverage and reserve. |
| First continuing operations/client-service employee | A real continuing workload, documented delivery process and a support function that frees both founders | At least $9,000/month attributable contribution for the $6,000 loaded role; positive company results including the role over a credible next-quarter forecast; reserve and history/backlog tests below. |
| Second collector/partnership or other continuing hire | Actual unanswered qualified demand or lost funded work persists after the first role/process works | Rebuild the budget. A $4,500 loaded role needs at least $6,750 new monthly contribution plus the same company-wide gates; it is not included in C. |

For a continuing hire, require all of the following:

1. **Work evidence:** time logs and a recurring bottleneck; ordinary process/automation improvements have been considered. Define the useful outcomes for the role.
2. **Contribution evidence:** new or protected revenue minus its incremental external delivery costs covers at least **1.5 times the fully loaded role cost**, without attributing the same contribution to several hires. This is a proposed management rule, not a guarantee.
3. **Company coverage:** at least three months of observed earned/collected operating performance, plus a costed next-quarter forecast including founder pay, existing staff and the proposed role. B's fully funded bounded term is the stated exception, not a substitute for a permanent-role test.
4. **Cash:** keep three months of the new full operating budget after setup, liabilities and the near-term obligations of prepaid services; identify six months of credible funding for the new role and required exit/transition obligations. A signed order still needs collection-risk assessment.
5. **Downside:** model a 20% receipts loss and loss of the largest material client/milestone. If either creates an unfunded gap, defer the permanent commitment, use a properly funded bounded scope, or obtain additional genuinely available financing.
6. **Delivery readiness:** a responsible manager, access controls, documented tasks and a measurable first 30/60/90-day plan.

C is **not yet an unconditional hiring approval**: its single-milestone loss test fails and would need more recurring contribution, stronger diversification, additional funded runway or a smaller commitment. Its first-role budget shows what hiring would cost, not proof the gates have been met.

At unchanged C revenue, adding the illustrative $4,500 second role leaves $4,784 before tax in the base month but turns the 20% receipts-loss result into **−$3,372.80**. A superficially positive base month is insufficient.

Classify workers from their actual relationship, not the preferred invoice label. Founder payments also depend on the chosen entity and tax treatment; partners are not automatically W-2 employees. Get the operating arrangement right before treating these gross compensation figures as payroll. [IRS: paying yourself](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/paying-yourself), [IRS: employee or independent contractor](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee).

## 13. Keep festival compensation and commercial ownership separate

A charitable festival has no founder profit pot to divide. It may pay reasonable compensation for real authorized work, with appropriate conflict handling and documentation. A separate company can have negotiated owners and profit arrangements, but cannot assume ownership of festival funds, marks, archives or contacts. [IRS: private benefit/inurement](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/inurement-private-benefit-charitable-organizations).

For insider compensation, the IRS describes a process supporting presumed reasonableness: advance approval by an authorized body without a conflict, appropriate comparable data and timely documentation. Actual arrangements require review; this document does not grant board authority. [IRS: compensation approval](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/rebuttable-presumption-intermediate-sanctions).

Any festival-to-company service payment needs its own approved scope, fair pricing, conflicts process and funding eligibility. Count it once in the correct entity; eliminate inter-entity transfers in any consolidated view. The same founder hours cannot be billed in full to both. Do not assume a grant pays commercial founder costs or that a commercial supporter receives a charitable deduction.

## 14. Decisions to make before choosing a scenario

| Decision | Required result |
|---|---|
| Personal feasibility, kept private | Each founder's minimum income, available hours and maximum cash risk. |
| Founder deal | Roles, compensation, ownership, earned interests, IP, financing, decision rights and departure/deadlock terms. |
| First offer | One finite service someone will actually buy, with delivery hours, rights, price and direct costs. |
| Funding | Named private funding sources, collection dates, permitted uses and reserve reconciliation; no public investor/buyer identities. |
| Operating scope | Actual seller, payment-cost subsidy, support coverage and content capacity. |
| First outside capacity | The smallest funded scope that removes the measured bottleneck, with proper worker classification. |
| Review | Monthly income/cost/cash review and a quarterly founder-pay/distribution decision; no unapproved automatic increases. |

**A proposal can begin with small paid work. Two dependable founder incomes require a demonstrated source of operating contribution and cash. A team requires additional contribution, management and resilience beyond that.**
