# Taos arts revival — PRD and executable business plan

**Version 1.0 · October 19, 2026 · Planning horizon: October 19, 2026–October 18, 2027**

**Status: a proposal for Stuart, Felix, and authorized partners.** No festival board approval, budget, partnership, staffing commitment, or 2027 festival date is assumed. The 2026 festival has already ended. The immediate task is to recover its evidence and build the next cycle from today.

## 1. The business we want to create

Make Taos an unusually rewarding place to discover and buy art, and make the resulting income meaningful to the people who create it. Combine an edited year-round guide and buying service with a revitalized Taos Fall Arts Festival. Start with Taos; earn the right to become a broader Southwest platform.

The principal product is **confidence and a useful relationship**: a buyer finds real available art, understands its significance and full cost, meets an informed person, purchases easily, and receives the work safely. The website, itinerary, festival, hotel introduction, and AI assistant are ways to deliver that service.

The mission is artist livelihood. Commercial discipline funds the service. Public programming opens the door to people who are not yet collectors. These purposes should be designed together rather than left to compete for leftover resources.

## 2. The problem and the market hypothesis

Artists need consistent access to buyers, respectful representation, practical selling support, and timely payment. Buyers need trusted selection, reliable information, transparent prices, and a frictionless path from interest to possession. Visitors need an intelligible introduction to a place whose art experiences are scattered. Galleries need relevant prospects and a service that respects their existing artist relationships. The festival needs operating capacity and comparable outcome data.

The hypothesis is that a coordinated service can convert more of the region's existing cultural interest into completed purchases, while attracting a deliberately cultivated set of new buyers. That claim must be tested. Neither global art-market value nor all Taos tourism spending is an addressable revenue estimate for this project.

### Initial audiences

| Audience | Need | First offer | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-time buyer, often below $1,500 | Confidence without embarrassment | Clear prices, a welcoming introduction, suitable original work | A satisfied first purchase and willingness to return. |
| Established collector, roughly $5,000–$25,000+ purchase interest | Distinctive work and credible access | Curated preview and a personal viewing | Qualified quote, completed purchase, repeat introduction. |
| Advisor, interior designer, architect | Dependable sourcing and specifications | Verified shortlist, trade coordination, installation support | Repeat paid orders with agreed fees. |
| Hotel or institutional procurement buyer | A coherent collection within a real budget | Paid brief, artist selection, phased delivery | Signed, funded milestones completed on time. |
| Artist or representing gallery | Useful demand without loss of control | Free core artist profile; verified inventory; opt-in referrals | Net proceeds, repeat buyers, fair treatment. |
| Resident, family, student | Belonging, discovery, learning, enjoyment | Free primary exhibitions, public programs, funded acquisitions | Meaningful participation and community support. |

Price bands describe service needs, not fixed judgments about people or artistic quality. Indigenous, Hispano, historical, modern, and experimental work need informed interpretation on their own terms.

## 3. The integrated offer

### A. Curated year-round guide

An edited entry point to art, artists, galleries, architecture, food, lodging, and selected experiences. Begin with a small set of excellent, verified choices. Publish the selection method and distinguish editorial choice from sponsorship. Core artist participation is free; no one can buy a recommendation disguised as independent judgment.

A Taos anchor prevents a regional brand from becoming a generic travel directory. Arroyo Seco can be included through a locally supported half-day route, real studio appointments, and accurate transport information. Do not imply that rural destinations are an easy extension of a downtown walk or direct crowds to private homes.

### B. Collector and procurement service

A human concierge gathers the buyer's interests, budget, dimensions, schedule, and delivery needs; proposes a short verified selection; arranges viewings; coordinates quotes; and follows the purchase through delivery. Private hospitality is optional. The art itself remains accessible to the public.

Build institutional and hospitality procurement as a repeatable second channel. A few substantial projects can diversify festival-season risk, but a speculative commission is not booked sales revenue. Pay artists for development when a buyer requests substantial custom work.

### C. Fall Arts 2027

Use a provisional late-September/early-October planning window until the festival board confirms dates and sites. PASEO publicly lists **September 17–18, 2027**; calendar coordination is an immediate opportunity, not an existing joint program. PASEO explicitly says its own program does not feature art booths or sales. [PASEO call](https://paseoproject.org/call-for-artists).

Proposed structure:

- A broad community exhibition with transparent eligibility and no artist application charge once underwriting is secured.
- An additional curated collector program with published criteria, invited appointments, and no purchased selection.
- A small number of high-quality public experiences: artist process demonstrations, school/community acquisitions, paid music where appropriate, and partner-led learning.
- Common inventory, wayfinding, staff training, purchase procedures, and fulfillment across participating sales venues.
- A preview period and post-festival follow-up, because some buyers decide before or after attending.

A sponsor-supported free-entry model is a **proposed change**, not a description of current rules. The existing festival participation page offers artist-submission sponsorships. [Current participation offer](https://taosfallarts.com/participate/).

### D. Cultural and public value

Create pathways for people to learn to choose art. A donor-funded student/community acquisition council can select real works for willing public institutions. Pay artists and cultural advisors. Offer professional development in pricing, documentation, buyer conversations, photography, and fulfillment. Build access through schedules, transport, seating, communication, and respectful treatment, not only free admission.

## 4. Outcomes and definitions

The base ambition is **$3 million in completed, attributable art sales** across the year, with a proposed $2 million festival-associated component and $1 million year-round component. Low and stretch scenarios are $1 million and $5 million. These are targets to validate, **not forecasts or promises**.

Track five outcome families:

1. **Art sold:** completed paid art value excluding tax, shipping, donations, tickets, refunded orders, and unfulfilled contract balances.
2. **Artist proceeds:** amounts actually paid or payable to artists, separately from gallery margin and project fees; also median and distribution.
3. **Operating sustainability:** unrestricted cash, earned income, grant restrictions, fully costed labor, and reserve needs.
4. **Public value:** free participation, resident experience, first buyers, learning, and accessibility.
5. **Trust:** inventory accuracy, response time, delivery, complaints, and payout reliability.

The base channel assumptions yield **$1.98 million artist proceeds before production costs and tax**, not artist profit. The operating model yields $579,000 income and $485,000 expenses before unresolved tax and exceptional costs. None of those figures is a historical result. Details and sensitivity are in the [financial plan](02-Financial-Model-and-Targets.md).

### Proposed operating scorecard

| Measure | Pilot, first 90 days | Base year-one ambition | Owner role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Verified, permissioned available works | 100 | Inventory supporting approximately $10m list value, validated by category | Inventory lead |
| Opted-in potential buyer households | 100 | 2,000 | Collector lead |
| Qualified buyer households | 20 | 800 | Collector lead |
| Buyer households with a completed purchase | Learn from first cohort | 440 | Sales operations |
| Completed orders | At least 5 | 550 | Sales operations |
| Completed art value | At least $25,000; test threshold | $3m | Finance |
| Artist payout service | All within agreed contract; target seven business days after clearance/acceptance | Same, with exceptions reported | Finance |
| Artwork availability accuracy | At least 95% in spot audit | At least 98% | Inventory lead |
| Inquiry first human response | One business day | One business day; staffed event desk during open hours | Concierge |
| Public program access | Funded pilot without artist fees | Free primary exhibition; access needs budgeted | Community lead |

The implied 440 buyers from 800 qualified households is **55% conversion**, an aggressive and unproven assumption. Stop presenting the base target as feasible if early cohorts do not support its inputs. “Qualified” means expressed budget, interest, timeframe, permission to follow up, and a plausible purchase opportunity—not wealth inferred from an address.

## 5. Business model and editorial independence

Earn from transparent transaction services, optional commercial partner services, sponsorship, eligible grants, patrons, and optional paid programs. A core artist profile and eligibility for editorial consideration should remain free. Paid commercial services can include photography, event production, verified catalog administration, concierge training, or clearly identified advertising.

Proposed direct-sale fees, gallery referrals, and commission-project fees differ because the service and seller responsibility differ. Existing representation agreements govern. No double commission, undisclosed referral charge, compulsory donation, or automatic transfer of a collector list is permitted by the proposed policy.

A gallery or restaurant may be editorially selected without paying. A paying business may still be excluded from an editorial recommendation. The commercial team cannot silently rewrite the curator's choices. Publish sponsor labels and a conflict register.

## 6. Governance and decision rights

The festival nonprofit and a possible separate regional business are distinct entities unless authorized leadership decides otherwise. Festival marks, archives, money, artist contacts, and charitable funds are not automatically assets of the new guide. Any shared services or data require a written agreement and fair allocation of costs and benefits.

Proposed roles, not appointments:

| Role | Accountability | Decision limit |
|---|---|---|
| Festival board / authorized officer | Festival mission, budget, contracts, grants, risk | Retains its legal authority; delegates explicitly. |
| Stuart, proposed product and business lead | Product, partner proposals, measurement, delivery coordination | No authority over nonprofit funds without delegation. |
| Felix, proposed curatorial and collector advisor | Artistic direction, artist/gallerist relationships | Recuses from House of Fire's selection and financial terms. |
| Festival producer | Venues, staffing, vendors, operating readiness | Within approved budget and contracts. |
| Collector lead / concierge | Buyer service, appointments, sales follow-up | Cannot promise discounts or authenticity beyond seller approval. |
| Finance / bookkeeping lead | Settlement, tax coordination, reporting, cash forecast | Dual approval for material payments; artist funds protected. |
| Paid artist/community advisory group | Artist experience, cultural specificity, access | Escalates concerns; participates in published selection procedures. |

Use three meetings: a 30-minute weekly operating review, a monthly finance/risk review, and a quarterly artist/community feedback session. Keep decisions, conflicts, and changes in a dated log. Appoint a complaints contact independent of a disputed sale's seller where feasible.

## 7. Build sequence and launch conditions

First sell through a manual service using a small, permissioned inventory. Build software after real interviews and transactions identify recurring work. The MVP is a useful catalog and inquiry-to-appointment flow, not an autonomous sales agent.

The first 90 days should produce a reconstructed 2026 baseline, a signed pilot scope, a few real transactions, quotes for the expensive operating items, a funding pipeline, and an honest go/no-go review. Do not commission a large print run, venue build, or regional advertising campaign before the catalog and response service work.

A festival expansion requires confirmed dates and authority, contracted sites, appropriate insurance and safety plans, a funded operating budget, protected artist payouts, a trained team, and adequate available work. Public programs require their own funding and responsible producers. Scale down rather than finance operations from artist money.

## 8. Risks that can invalidate the plan

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers do not convert at assumed rates | Meetings without quotes or deposits | Interview lost buyers, change inventory/service, lower sales case. |
| Too little suitable work at higher price points | Buyers' shortlists cannot be fulfilled | Recruit appropriate inventory; do not inflate valuations. |
| Sponsor/grant money arrives late or never | Unfunded commitments in the cash forecast | Stage contracts, obtain actual cash, reduce scope. |
| Reliance on one curator or two unpaid founders | Slow replies, missed tasks | Narrow scope, pay core roles, document handoffs and backups. |
| Cultural or editorial trust is lost | Complaints, undisclosed paid selection | Independent review, corrections, recusal, paid cultural guidance. |
| Artists sell but are not paid promptly | Unreconciled settlements | Separate payout ledger, stop expansion, resolve immediately. |
| Travel, weather, fire, or site failure | Bookings or readiness deteriorate | Remote viewings, alternate indoor program, cancellation plan. |
| Public enjoyment crowds out buying | High attendance, low qualified inquiries | Separate quiet viewings, clear buying service, better matching. |
| Commercial/nonprofit relationship is improper | Unclear ownership and money flows | Written agreement and legal/accounting review before transfers. |

## 9. What success looks like after one year

A serious result is not merely a redesigned website. It is a defensible sales ledger, paid artists, returning buyers, an engaged public, a financially supported team, reliable partners, and a festival that can explain what it accomplished. A smaller pilot that proves those mechanisms is more useful than an expensive event that cannot pay its bills.

If the base case proves attainable, reinvest surplus under the correct entity's authority in artist access, staff continuity, reserves, and buyer development. Housing and studio affordability may become a longer-term partnership agenda; this year's plan does not claim that a festival alone can solve them.
