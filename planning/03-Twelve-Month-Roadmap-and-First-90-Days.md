## First 30 days: by November 17

- Reconcile 2026 art sold, artist payouts, organizational income, attendance definitions, and unresolved liabilities as far as records allow.
- Conduct 12 artist/gallery interviews, ten buyer interviews, five hospitality/advisor interviews, and three public/community listening sessions or small groups. Pay cultural advisors and budget participation support where needed.
- Select a pilot with approximately ten artist/gallery suppliers and 100 permissioned works. Audit price, rights, representation, availability, and readiness to ship.
- Draft seller/referral agreements, editorial policy, privacy notice, and a simple buyer brief. Identify tax, insurance, and payment responsibilities before taking money.
- Obtain actual quotes for venue, insurance, handling, production, and core paid roles. The financial model remains an estimate until then.
- Recruit 100 opt-in potential buyer households through consented introductions. Start with Taos and a small Denver feeder-market test.
- Run one small preview or appointment session and record the whole funnel, including why people decline.

**Gate 1 — November 17:** continue if authority, a viable seller/settlement arrangement, credible inventory, an accountable operator, and funded pilot capacity exist. Otherwise narrow to research and introductions until the missing conditions are resolved. A proposed first-90-day cash ceiling is **$15,000**, staged rather than automatically spent.

## Days 31–60: November 18–December 17

Deliver a mobile catalog and manual concierge; do not begin a large custom build. Pilot two acquisition channels with capped spending. Offer tailored shortlists, in-person or remote viewings, and full delivery quotes. Test one paid commercial service with a clear deliverable rather than selling a vague future audience.

Prepare the final grant application early enough for the authorized applicant to review artistic samples, governance, budget, accessibility, and public benefit. Do not imply that grant funding is available for unrestricted commercial acquisition spending.

**Gate 2 — December 17:** retain channels producing qualified inquiries and useful appointments. Pause spending that produces only low-intent traffic. Review whether fee revenue exceeds the incremental cost of serving completed transactions. Keep the public mission's subsidized work separately visible.

## Days 61–90: December 18–January 17

- **December 18, 2026, 11:59pm MT:** New Mexico Arts final application deadline, if eligible and authorized.
- Complete at least five paid pilot orders and at least $25,000 art value as proposed learning thresholds; report shortfalls rather than stretching definitions.
- Target 20 qualified buyer households and at least ten useful viewing appointments. Record the denominator and cohort dates.
- Reconcile every fee, artist payment, refund, and delivery. Interview successful and lost buyers.
- Test the HeyTaos pilot only if terms and content permissions are agreed.
- Produce a revised 2027 sales case based on actual inventory, conversion, staffing capacity, and quotes.

**Gate 3 — January 17:** a five-sale pilot does not prove a $3m business. It earns the next experiment only if the service works, artist outcomes are sound, and the next stage has funding. Choose low, base, or paused-expansion scope explicitly.

## Funding and readiness gates

**Before each new commitment:** maintain a rolling 13-week cash forecast, match funding restrictions to expenses, and protect all artist, tax, refund, and shipping liabilities. Count only cleared cash and sufficiently reliable collectible commitments under finance review. Do not treat the modeled grant or art-sales targets as available money.

**By July 15:** confirm all non-cancellable commitments can be paid and that an operating reserve covers at least eight weeks of unavoidable costs. Obtain cancellation terms. If funding or staffing is inadequate, reduce venues, paid promotion, and optional events.

**Thirty days before opening:** sites and dates are confirmed; safety and access responsibilities are signed off by responsible professionals; insurance and seller agreements are in place; inventory audit passes; payment and settlement are rehearsed; contingency communications are ready. A spreadsheet marked “done” is not a substitute for those confirmations.

## Weekly operating rhythm

Monday: review qualified demand, inventory gaps, quotes, paid sales, and cash. Wednesday: resolve stalled orders and partner issues. Friday: reconcile payments, update next week's appointments, check response time, and publish a short decision log. Monthly: review artist distribution and public access alongside revenue.

Use the [weekly scorecard](../templates/Weekly-Scorecard.md). If founder availability is below ten hours a week each and no paid operator is funded, constrain the work to a small concierge pilot; the full festival plan requires a dedicated team.
