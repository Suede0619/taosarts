# Paying the team and financing the work before opening

**October 24, 2026. Analytical options, not approved compensation, equity, hiring or fundraising terms.** Read alongside the earlier [founder-compensation scenarios](../../planning/09-Founder-Compensation-and-Hiring-Scenarios.md), which do not finance this Denver event. Named working-role recommendations remain local/private.

## A share of proceeds can be useful; it cannot be the whole production plan

The preferable structure is **funded pay for defined work, with a carefully defined additional success pool**. Founders can choose entrepreneurial risk in their own venture. Asking every contractor, employee, artist and supplier to absorb that risk would narrow the available talent pool and exclude people without independent financial support.

A major studio may accept a royalty, revenue participation or co-production arrangement. That is something to negotiate alongside rights, guarantees, deposits, travel and technical costs—not evidence that it will finance this event. No researched candidate has offered to work for future proceeds.

| Compensation method | Appropriate use | Weakness / required protection |
|---|---|---|
| Paid fixed-scope fee | Feasibility, brand brief, code review, specialist advice and other bounded deliverables | Define acceptance, schedule, expenses, IP and termination; employee classification still depends on facts |
| Funded retainer or salary | Ongoing work with dependable capacity | Budget the full employment/contracting cost, applicable wage obligations and taxes/coverage |
| Base fee plus milestone payments | Producer, design, marketing and technical phases | Milestones must be within the role's control; committed funds must cover contracted obligations |
| Base fee plus defined incentive | Sponsor sales, collector conversion, delivery performance or a team bonus | State the eligible metric, attribution, refund period, cap, payment date and conflict controls |
| Genuine founder equity/vesting | Long-lived responsibility, capital and continuing company contribution | Not the same as an event bonus; requires ownership, governance, vesting, IP and departure terms |
| Deferred contractor fee | Limited, knowingly accepted commercial credit from a genuine independent business | Creates debt even if the fair loses money unless expressly contingent; quantify exposure and cancellation treatment |
| Contingent profit participation | Extra upside after the agreed settlement waterfall | Could pay zero; define the pool and do not present it as guaranteed compensation |

The US Department of Labor explains that employees cannot volunteer services to for-profit private-sector employers. Colorado labor guidance looks at the actual working relationship, not merely a contract label or a 1099. Do not build the commercial fair's operating model around “volunteers,” interns or purported contractors doing employee work without proper pay. Genuine founder/owner arrangements and nonprofit volunteering require their own fact-specific treatment. [US DOL](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/docs/volunteers.asp), [Colorado classification guidance](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%2310_who_is_and_isn%27t_an_employee_08.11.25.pdf)

## Three practical approaches

| Approach | How people get paid | Seed requirement | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Bounded founder discovery | Founders choose limited initial unpaid/equity work; essential outside expertise is paid | Enough cash for indispensable assessment; strict time/cash ceiling | Suitable only for a short, consciously limited investigation. Do not promise a full fair or recruit an unpaid professional team. |
| **B. Underwritten paid feasibility, then funded delivery** | Defined initial fees; later paid contracts released only after site/funding gates; optional upside | A dedicated feasibility underwriting commitment, followed by a separate funded delivery/cash plan | **Recommended research direction.** Offers a concrete deliverable to an early backer and pays for the evidence needed to price the fair. |
| C. Experienced co-producer partnership | Professional operator receives funded base/approved costs and negotiated upside; founders contribute their specialist functions | Still needs deposits, cash commitments and loss/cancellation allocation | Can improve execution credibility and access; likely reduces founder control/profit and requires careful rights/governance terms. No such operator is committed. |

In all three, protect seller money and fund work before it is contracted. None guarantees owner or founder profit.

## How an upside pool could work

First pay legitimate work and protect liabilities. Then define a pool from **actual distributable event profit**, after seller settlement, refunds/claims, applicable tax provision, agreed reserves and documented financing/venue priorities. No percentage allocation among founders, the owner, investors or staff is agreed.

For illustration only, if the professionally reviewed eligible pool were $100,000 and an agreed team bonus pool were 20%, the pool would be $20,000. Allocation would follow documented responsibilities/performance, not an improvised promise of a slice of artwork GMV. The $100,000 example is independent of the event model, which does not calculate actual tax or distributable profit.

Distinguish company equity, founder service fees, sponsor-sales commissions, employee bonuses, vendor fees and owner participation. Multiple percentages all charged to “proceeds” can over-allocate the same dollar. Agree whether venue participation is before or after a bonus pool and how approved founder fees affect profit; independent review of related-party fees protects trust.

For any seller-sales incentive, preserve representation agreements and disclose the payer and basis. For sponsorship, define whether compensation is on contracted value or **collected eligible cash**, including refunds, renewals and attribution. Charitable fundraising compensation may raise different rules/ethics; do not copy a commercial sales commission into a grant/donation relationship without review.

DDA's general process is rolling, with published review estimates of 30–90 days plus another 30–90 for approval; a conditional award still requires a contract. Construction gap financing for the owner's property is a different transaction from an art-fair grant. The current arts category does not establish that ordinary private indoor event operations qualify.

The public funding question is therefore **“Which defined public benefit, expense and applicant actually qualify?”**, not simply “Will the city seed the company?” Eligible artist/staff work can sometimes be funded where a program allows it. Restricted awards cannot automatically become founder distributions or a developer profit share; each agreement controls eligible expenses, timing and reporting.

Debt or investment has different obligations from sponsorship. Selling passive interests in future profits may be a securities transaction; no investment instrument, return or offering is designed here. Use the appropriate structure and advice before soliciting capital. [SEC small-business terminology](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/glossary)

## When to bring people on

1. **Before any funded stage:** agree founding responsibilities, capacity, limited discovery exposure and who may make commitments. Keep the scope within available time/cash.
2. **Once paid feasibility funds are secured:** commission the producer, code specialist, fractional finance and collector/sponsor assessment. Pay the brand/founding scopes against agreed deliverables.
3. **Only after a credible site and financing path:** contract the core delivery team, gallery/artist services and appropriate creative commissions. Fund deposits and cancellation liabilities first.
4. **Only after the launch gates are met:** expand campaign, installation, registrar, visitor/security/access and show staffing against the approved footprint and public hours.
5. **After close and reconciliation:** settle sellers/contractors, satisfy liabilities/reserves and distribute any properly defined upside. An apparent bank balance on opening night is not distributable profit.

If seed funding does not materialize, reduce the paid investigation to a genuinely affordable scope or pause further commitments. Do not finance the gap through unlimited unpaid work or seller proceeds. CAFTA may provide an eligibility-based legal referral; it is not retained counsel or guaranteed free service. [Colorado Attorneys for the Arts](https://cbca.org/colorado-attorneys-for-the-arts/)
