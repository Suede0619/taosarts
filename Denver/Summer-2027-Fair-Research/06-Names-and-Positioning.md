# Naming and positioning exploration

**October 24, 2026. Creative research only.** No name is selected, trademark-cleared, registered, licensed or publicly announced. Searches below are preliminary web screens, not a complete trademark/common-law/domain/social review. The working Southwest Art platform brand remains provisional.

## Naming recommendation to test

**COMMON ALTITUDE** is the leading working direction, with a plain descriptor such as **Denver Art Fair & Exhibition**.

“Common” supports public welcome and shared cultural value; “altitude” anchors Denver without requiring a Western/Southwest art style. It can contain serious gallery work and ambitious installations without sounding like a private condominium promotion or a music-only festival. The name can survive a smaller approved footprint and later editions elsewhere; it does not require all tower floors to be open.

This is an expert creative judgment, not audience-tested preference or evidence of legal availability. Exact-phrase and art/festival web searches did not surface an obvious identical art-fair brand in the reviewed results; technical aviation uses of the words did appear. That is weak screening evidence, not clearance. Test it with collectors, artists, local residents and the host before investing in identity assets.

| Working name | Strength | Weakness / preliminary search result | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|

| **OPEN FLOORS — Denver** | Immediately communicates invitation and building transformation | “Open floor” already occurs in art and dance contexts, including a WUK exhibition; less distinctive and potentially confusing | Strong alternative, subject to deeper review |
| **VERTICAL COMMONS** | A compelling civic/architectural idea for a multi-floor exhibition | Existing architecture/thesis use; sounds less like a place to buy art and depends on the eventual site concept | Possible edition/theme, not current first choice |
| **RISING ROOMS** | Warm, spatial and optimistic | An existing community uses “Rising Rooms”; music use also surfaced; could sound like hospitality/interiors | Secondary creative territory, not preferred |
| **AFTER OFFICE** | Explains office reuse and the shift from work to cultural life | Very generic; existing “After Office Hours” communities and common event phrasing; may imply evening-only entertainment | Better editorial/program wording than master brand |

## Position the promise before designing the logo

A useful working proposition to evaluate is: **a remarkable place to discover and acquire contemporary art, with a public cultural experience that belongs to the city**. It is broader than a regional style and more precise than “a sensation.” It leaves room for painting, sculpture, photography, craft where curatorially justified, digital work and performance without promising every category.

Different audiences need different evidence:

| Audience | Reason to care | Proof the eventual communication needs |
|---|---|---|
| Serious collector | Distinctive work, trusted selection and dependable purchase/delivery | Credible exhibitors, artwork information, appointments, provenance, service and fulfillment |
| Artist | Real earning opportunity and respectful treatment | Clear terms, accessible participation, fees/payment support, rights and timely settlement |
| Local visitor | A welcoming cultural place worth exploring | Good art, understandable information, accessible routes and meaningful public entry |
| Developer | A useful activation aligned with redevelopment | Available/approved footprint, measurable rental/commercial interest, controlled cost and construction protection |
| Sponsor | Defined public/client value and association with excellent work | Concrete rights, appropriate audience, truthful metrics and delivery capacity |
| City/community | Paid creative work and a well-run public benefit | Local accountability, safe/accessibly operated space, neighborhood coordination and transparent use of public support |

Do not lead publicly with “rivaling Art Basel.” Keep that ambition as a quality and long-term scale reference. Leading with an established fair's name invites comparison on decades of relationships, selection and transaction confidence that a first edition has not yet earned. Nor should commercial art sales be hidden beneath language suggesting this is simply a free municipal festival.

## Ideas worth testing, not approved program features

- **One acquisition experience across physical and digital discovery:** a consistent artwork record, clear seller authority, excellent images, appointment support and follow-through after the visit. Deliver trustworthy service before adding complicated technology.
- **Paid acquisition briefs for the built environment:** a curated route for hospitality, office and residential-development clients to commission or acquire work. Test actual buyer needs; corporate art demand differs from private-collector demand.
- **A public cultural layer funded in advance:** sponsor-underwritten public access, paid artist conversations or local commissions, with a visible budget and outcomes. Avoid funding “free access” through undisclosed deductions from seller receipts.
- **A genuinely useful artist-outcome report:** completed sales, seller settlement time, concentration of sales and paid opportunities, published only as cleared aggregates. This is stronger mission evidence than crowd photographs alone.
- **A Southwest collector itinerary beyond the fair:** carefully selected visits and onward art travel, evaluated only after the core event works. It does not expand Taos-only website v1 or establish partner agreements.

These are combinations of known approaches to explore, not claims of unprecedented invention. Site engineering, rights, capacity and cash determine what can be delivered.
