# Developer strategy, rental housing and existing cultural events

**October 24, 2026.** Evidence below distinguishes current public direction, reported schedules, advertised activity and unresolved owner consent. Earlier acquisition and budget detail remains in the [Denver discovery package](../03-Denver-Acquisitions-and-Conversion-Timeline.md).

## Rentals are the current direction

In the June 11, 2026 Ross Kaminsky interview, the developer is asked directly whether the planned housing is rental or condominium. The discussion at approximately 03:12–04:24 describes rental housing as the current approach, while acknowledging that strategy could theoretically change. It also describes retaining one of the four acquired towers as office space. This is a first-party statement of intent, not a permanent legal prohibition on later condominium conversion. [Primary interview](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/759-the-ross-kaminsky-show-20710514/episode/6-11-26-interview-asher-luzzatto-can-he-336567593/)

The Energy Center owner's current website separately presents a commercial tower and a residential tower, including service to existing and prospective office tenants. That contradicts a blanket assumption that the whole complex is empty and available for event use. [Owner website](https://www.denverenergycenter.com/home)

The current first-party/public record does **not** provide the developer's full investment underwriting: target returns, construction financing close, permanent debt terms, rents, operating margins and disposition horizon are unknown. The plausible inference is a conversion-and-leasing strategy that creates income and property value, not evidence that a quick property sale is the primary exit. The owner could still refinance or dispose of assets later; no hold period is established here.

High Fidelity's publicly described development estimate is approximately $315m, with a board-approved **up to $63m low-interest DDA loan**. That is redevelopment support, not available cash for this art fair. Mixed-income aspirations also do not mean every apartment is income restricted. [City announcement](https://www.denvergov.org/News-articles/2026/DDDA-Approves-Funding-for-High-Fidelity-Plaza-Downtown-Safety-Action-Plan)

Energy Center's reported development estimates are $145m–$150m depending on source/date. The separate $29m DDA figure in earlier discovery is a **request**, not an established award. These amounts should not appear in a deck as event sponsorship or owner liquidity. See the [prior acquisition/finance reconciliation](../03-Denver-Acquisitions-and-Conversion-Timeline.md).

## What an art fair could contribute to that strategy

These are proposed benefits to validate, not promised financial returns:

| Host objective | Useful fair contribution | Evidence / metric needed | What would overstate it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build a credible destination identity | Excellent art, public programming and a coherent account of the site's future | Independent coverage quality, visitor feedback, approved content and local partner response | Guaranteed worldwide headlines |
| Future rental demand | Optional, clearly separate interest registration for housing updates | Consented qualified inquiries; later tour and application conversion, measured by owner | Treating every attendee as a future resident or condominium buyer |
| Office and commercial leasing | Hosted visits for appropriate prospective tenants | Attributable broker introductions, qualified requirements and follow-up | Claiming leases will result without term sheets or attribution |
| Retail/community activation | Public access that supports nearby businesses | Counting method, local spending survey, partner participation and accessibility outcomes | Unverified economic-impact multipliers |
| Reduce perceived vacancy | Temporary activity that demonstrates a cared-for place | Approved footprint, owner operating cost, restoration and tenant satisfaction | Assuming occupancy legally changes the building's permitted use |
| Generate event income | Agreed share of a precisely defined organizer income or profit pool | Contractual definition, waterfall, audit and timing | Charging an owner share against money owed to artists |

Residential delivery may be years after the fair. An interest list is not a near-term lease pipeline unless the developer defines when it will accept applications. Collect event registration and optional real-estate interest separately; agree retention, sharing, opt-out and ownership of the data. No attendee or collector list is promised to the landlord.

## The summer 2027 conflict

September 14 reporting places Energy Center conversion construction from **Q1 2027 to early 2029**. October 22 reporting still describes permits as expected by late 2026. Applications, expectations and published construction intentions are not issued permits or an available event license. [Permit/construction reporting](https://www.bisnow.com/news/denver/multifamily/developer-applies-for-construction-permit-for-downtown-denver-office-to-residential-project), [October 22 ULI coverage](https://milehighcre.com/uli-panel-explores-the-future-of-downtown-beyond-office-conversions/)

Before any credible owner economics can be priced, obtain an owner-approved schedule showing: address; floors; concourse ownership/boundary; construction separation; tenant circulation; load-in/out; preparation, public and restoration periods; utilities; life-safety systems; and an alternative if possession changes. Gross building area is not usable exhibition area or approved attendance capacity.

The concourse's description as a “mall” remains unverified. Do not combine it with the public 16th Street corridor, Denver Pavilions or another owner's property. The feasibility question is whether a defined, insurable and permitted portion can host the event without delaying construction. There is no evidence yet that both buildings can be given over to a fair.
