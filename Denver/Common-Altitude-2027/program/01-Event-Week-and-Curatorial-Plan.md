# Proposed event week and curatorial program

**Planning dates: August 9–15, 2027, America/Denver.** No dates, spaces, sessions, participants or capacities are booked. Public art days are August 11–15, proposed 10:00–19:00. The full [106-record proposed schedule](../data/event-program.json) is operational draft data, not a public calendar announcement.

## Why seven days

Two professional/preview days create time for seller/artist orientation, press, advisers and meaningful appointments. Five public days support local attendance, school/community access where appropriate, collector follow-up and a strong weekend. This is a first-edition format hypothesis; if site, team or cash cannot support seven days, recost a shorter edition rather than simply deleting preparation/strike.

Load-in is proposed from July 26, final readiness August 8, strike/restoration August 16–20. Owner possession, contractor interfaces and required inspections control every date. The undercroft/concourse and both towers are not assumed available.

The August week avoids the confirmed Cherry Creek July 2–4 and Cheesman Park July 24–25 weekends. It is not a complete conflict clearance: verify owner construction, Denver events/hotels, Aspen, Santa Fe, school/community calendars and collector travel before holding a date. [Cherry Creek](https://cherryarts.org/events/cherry-creek-arts-festival/faq/), [Cheesman Park](https://dashevents.com/productions/cheesman-park-art-fest/)

## Curatorial argument

**Office of the Impossible** asks what art can make possible in a place built for ordinary work. Let individual artists answer with their own work. Rooms should be distinct artistic propositions, not identical booths or a theme park of borrowed famous styles.

Recommended balance: 24 gallery presentations; 12 direct-artist salons; six signature briefs; six learning/program spaces; six common/public spaces; six service/support spaces. This is a functional inventory to map to real approved space. It is not a floor plan or capacity calculation.

Gallery selection should balance artistic quality, trusted seller practices, media/price range, local relevance and outside perspectives. Target a useful mix of Denver/Front Range, wider North America and international participation without quotas that force weak or unavailable work. Emerging artists receive proper presentation and seller support.

## Standard art-fair functions delivered well

An edited catalogue; clear seller/artist labels; collector/adviser appointments; press preview; artist talks; education; public opening; buyer care; artwork handling/condition tracking; packing/shipping coordination; information/help; accessible toilets/rest/quiet routes; staffed security/medical; orderly load-in/strike; and financial/outcome reconciliation.

The first edition must do these reliably before increasing spectacle. An art-market desk should be as well designed as a signature room.

## Guided discovery and premium-program capacity

The $300,000 premium-program illustration assumes 6,000 unique buyers at $50 average, not six thousand people in a single evening. Proposed product: one reserved, staffed 45-minute discovery journey plus clearly defined guide/content benefits. Core art remains available without the paid guide.

Planning inventory is **96 staggered departures per public day × 15 seats × five days = 7,200 seat-visits**. Depart every five minutes from 10:00 to 17:55; final journey ends 18:40. Twelve suitably staffed guide positions/rotations would be tested against the real site, breaks, route interference and paid labor plan. It does not establish lawful occupant load or a deliverable 7,200 slots.

Selling 6,000 of 7,200 proposed seats is an aggressive demand/utilization assumption. Test willingness to pay and guide quality early. If the price, capacity or uptake fails, revise income/funding rather than overfill routes. Final reservations cannot open until actual capacity and delivery are approved.

## Learning program

Two proposed workshop rooms each run five 60-minute sessions per public day, with turnover and a lunch gap. At a provisional 20 seats, that yields **50 sessions / 1,000 paid seats**. Times: 10:00, 11:30, 13:30, 15:00 and 16:30. $35 is a price hypothesis; all seats are not guaranteed sold.

A separate free open studio operates in two supervised blocks daily. Public talks are free. Any scholarship/purchase subsidy needs explicit funding and accounting: a sponsored seat cannot also be counted as a full-price customer payment unless the sponsor actually purchases it in a separate correctly classified transaction.

Teaching artists are paid. Draft topics include drawing, small editions, material experiments, image/object processes and beginner collecting. Think 360, ASLD or qualified independent educators are candidates; no instructor is booked. Youth participation needs age-appropriate materials, caregiver/supervision and safeguarding procedures. It is not unpaid staffing.

## Evening program

Three proposed evening slots occupy a separately approved zone after ordinary gallery hours. Plan an artist-authored light/sound encounter, a live process/performance evening and a community/collaborative closing-night experience. Human Resources, Threyda and commissioned artists are possible collaborators, not booked acts.

Sound, flashing light, late transport, neighbor impact, alcohol/food licensing if any, emergency access, staffing and sensory alternatives need specific review. Daytime art viewing and sale conversations must remain calm and intelligible. No outdoor projection, pyrotechnic, drone or water work is assumed.

## Selection, schedule and program control

The artistic lead owns content and selection; education owns learning/safeguarding; the producer owns site/run-of-show; collector/registrar own appointments and work availability; finance approves funded obligations; brand/PR publish only agreed facts.

Every session/work has an accepted counterpart, fee, rights/recording terms, technical rider, access/age information, capacity, setup/strike, insurance responsibility and backup. A public program entry is not created from a famous name on the research longlist.
