# Proposed event week and curatorial program

**Planning dates: August 9–15, 2027, America/Denver.** The project has no bookings for dates, spaces, sessions or participants and no approved capacity. Public art days are August 11–15, proposed 10:00–19:00. The [106-record schedule](../data/event-program.json) supports internal planning. The project has announced no public calendar.

## Why seven days

Two professional/preview days create time for seller/artist orientation, press, advisers and meaningful appointments. Five public days support local attendance, school/community access where appropriate, collector follow-up and a strong weekend. This is a first-edition format hypothesis. If site, team or cash cannot support seven days, price a shorter edition with preparation and strike costs intact.

The plan schedules load-in from July 26, final readiness on August 8 and strike/restoration on August 16–20. Owner possession, contractor interfaces and required inspections control every date. The undercroft/concourse and both towers are not assumed available.

The August week avoids the confirmed Cherry Creek July 2–4 and Cheesman Park July 24–25 weekends. It is not a complete conflict clearance: verify owner construction, Denver events/hotels, Aspen, Santa Fe, school/community calendars and collector travel before holding a date. [Cherry Creek](https://cherryarts.org/events/cherry-creek-arts-festival/faq/), [Cheesman Park](https://dashevents.com/productions/cheesman-park-art-fest/)

## Curatorial argument

**Office of the Impossible** asks what art can make possible in a place built for ordinary work. Let individual artists answer with their own work. Each room needs an artist’s or curator’s specific proposal. Avoid copying famous artists’ styles.

Recommended balance: 24 gallery presentations. 12 direct-artist salons. Six signature briefs. Six learning/program spaces. Six common/public spaces. Six service/support spaces. This is a functional inventory to map to real approved space. It is not a floor plan or capacity calculation.

Gallery selection should balance artistic quality, trusted seller practices, media/price range, local relevance and outside perspectives. Target a useful mix of Denver/Front Range, wider North America and international participation without quotas that force weak or unavailable work. Emerging artists receive proper presentation and seller support.

## Standard art-fair functions delivered well

The standard program needs a catalog, seller labels, appointments, press preview, talks, learning and a public opening. Operations must cover buyer care, art handling, condition records, packing, shipping and information desks. Provide accessible toilets, rest areas and quiet routes. Budget security, medical staff, load-in, strike and financial reconciliation.

The first edition must do these reliably before increasing spectacle. An art-market desk should be as well designed as a signature room.

## Guided discovery and premium-program capacity

The $300,000 premium-program calculation assumes 6,000 unique buyers at a $50 average over the event. Proposed product: one reserved, staffed 45-minute discovery journey plus clearly defined guide/content benefits. Core art remains available without the paid guide.

Planning inventory is **96 staggered departures per public day × 15 seats × five days = 7,200 seat-visits**. Depart every five minutes from 10:00 to 17:55. Final journey ends 18:40. Test twelve guide positions and rotations against the actual site, breaks, route conflicts and labor budget. It does not establish lawful occupant load or a deliverable 7,200 slots.

Selling 6,000 of 7,200 proposed seats is an aggressive demand/utilization assumption. Test willingness to pay and guide quality early. If price, capacity or uptake falls short, revise income and funding. Keep routes within approved capacity. Open reservations only after the responsible parties approve capacity and delivery.

## Learning program

Two proposed workshop rooms each run five 60-minute sessions per public day, with turnover and a lunch gap. At a provisional 20 seats, that yields **50 sessions / 1,000 paid seats**. Times: 10:00, 11:30, 13:30, 15:00 and 16:30. $35 is a price hypothesis. All seats are not guaranteed sold.

A separate free open studio operates in two supervised blocks daily. Public talks are free. Fund and account for purchase or seat subsidies explicitly. Count sponsor-purchased seats once under the correct revenue category.

Pay teaching artists. Draft topics include drawing, small editions, material experiments, image/object processes and beginner collecting. Think 360, ASLD or qualified independent educators are candidates. The project has booked no instructor. Youth participation needs age-appropriate materials, caregiver/supervision and safeguarding procedures. It is not unpaid staffing.

## Evening program

Three proposed evening slots occupy a separately approved zone after ordinary gallery hours. Plan an artist-authored light/sound encounter, a live process/performance evening and a community/collaborative closing-night experience. Human Resources, Threyda and commissioned artists are candidates. The project has booked no act.

Sound, flashing light, late transport, neighbor impact, alcohol/food licensing if any, emergency access, staffing and sensory alternatives need specific review. Daytime art viewing and sale conversations must remain calm and intelligible. The base scope includes no outdoor projection, pyrotechnics, drones or water work.

## Selection, schedule and program control

The artistic lead owns content and selection. Education owns learning/safeguarding. The producer owns site/run-of-show. Collector/registrar own appointments and work availability. Finance approves funded obligations. Brand/PR publish only agreed facts.

Every session/work has an accepted counterpart, fee, rights/recording terms, technical rider, access/age information, capacity, setup/strike, insurance responsibility and backup. A public program entry is not created from a famous name on the research longlist.
