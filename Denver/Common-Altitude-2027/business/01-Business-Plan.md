# COMMON ALTITUDE 2027: proposed business plan

**Version 0.1, October 24, 2026.** This plan proposes a first edition. The event has no approval, validated forecast or commercial offer. All commitments remain unmade. The governing numerical inputs are [project.json](../data/project.json) and [generated finance](../data/financial-results.md).

## Executive proposition

Create a seven-day art fair/exhibition in a carefully approved Denver adaptive-reuse site: **COMMON ALTITUDE / OFFICE OF THE IMPOSSIBLE**. Two professional/preview days lead into five free core-art discovery days, with optional paid special experiences and workshops. Serious buying service and exceptional public programming share one coherent identity.

The proposed planning week is **August 9–15, 2027**, with public art days August 11–15. No venue/date hold exists. The researched owner's conversion construction overlaps summer 2027, so site feasibility is the first commercial dependency. The two-tower/hundreds-of-rooms concept remains the expanded ambition. The recommended costed planning unit is **60 programmed rooms or equivalent spaces**, with 24- and 120-space alternatives.

The purpose is to generate completed art purchases and paid creative work that help sustain artists, while expanding the audience for art. The event supports the wider regional platform mission. It does not replace Taos-only website v1 or authorize a new website build.

## Market context and positioning

The Art Basel/UBS annual report estimates global 2025 art sales of $59.6bn, up 4%. The global figure gives market context. It does not estimate Denver demand or this event’s revenue. The 2025 HNW collecting survey shows the relevance of galleries, fairs and artist-direct channels within its selected sample. Use the survey to design buyer service. Validate local demand before using spending assumptions in a forecast. [Annual report](https://www.artbasel.com/news/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026), [dated collector channels](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/survey-of-global-collecting-2025/channels)

Denver already has respected arts festivals, galleries, museums, digital/public-art programs and immersive attractions. The gap to test is a coherent combination of an edited acquisition market, a remarkable multi-room exhibition, paid local participation and exceptional buying service at a useful scale. Do not claim the city lacks art or that established events need replacing.

The program includes contemporary art and other work across styles. First edition credibility comes from the quality of selected work, verified seller authority, the producer's competence and actual collector demand. Use Art Basel as a reference for service and operating discipline. The project has no affiliation with it.

The counts allocate program functions. A survey and use approval must establish the rooms and capacity. A signature room may need much more area than an office. Some functions may share space. No gross-square-foot-to-attendance calculation authorizes public use.

Proposed inventory target: **300 cleared saleable artwork records**. 192 from 24 gallery presentations, 72 from 12 direct-artist salons and 36 editions/small works. Actual acceptance and availability are unknown. Signature commissions/loans may be additional and not for sale. Do not total scraped asking prices into claimed inventory value.

## Revenue and artist economics

The proposal assumes **zero organizer commission on artwork sales**. Artist/gallery representation, payment processing, tax, shipping and external seller costs remain separate and disclosed. The parties still need to agree on the physical-fair structure. The existing direct-artist website zero-commission promise remains intact.

| Core organizer income | Assumption | Proposed gross |
|---|---|---:|
| Sponsorship | 17-package illustration, subject to actual demand and rights | $1,250,000 |
| Premium programs | 6,000 unique paid-program buyers × $50 average, within 12,000 total unique visitors | $300,000 |
| Optional gallery services | 24 purchases × $7,500 | $180,000 |
| Paid workshops | 1,000 paid seats × $35. Workshop buyers may overlap premium buyers | $35,000 |
| Other earned services/publications | Service and publication income assumption without customer evidence | $50,000 |
| **Organizer income** | Excludes artwork seller balances, tax and restricted funds | **$1,815,000** |

Core gallery/artist selection is independent of payment. Optional services must have demonstrable value. The model's 100% uptake by 24 galleries is a strong unvalidated assumption. Define included handling, documentation or concierge support precisely. Participation cannot secretly become a mandatory fee to make the model work.

Core art target: **600 qualified collector households × 25% completed-purchase conversion × $20,000 average completed order = $3m**. Assume one order per purchasing household for this illustration. This is not a proven buyer list or forecast. Attendance, deposits and asking prices are not completed art GMV.

## Costs and pay

The proposed core cost envelope is **$1,550,000**, including $70,000 paid feasibility. It contains $260,000 core-team fees, $200,000 artist/program fees, $290,000 production/fit-out, $100,000 building operations, $120,000 art logistics, $100,000 visitor/security/access, $180,000 marketing/collector/PR, $65,000 professional/insurance/finance allowance and $165,000 contingency. Obtain quotes before commitment.

Model later core-team allocations are artistic leadership $75,000, product/program $75,000, fractional brand $35,000, executive producer $55,000 and fractional finance $20,000. These amounts budget defined scopes. They establish no salary, appointment, worker classification or unlimited hours. They do not purchase unlimited hours. Feasibility fees are separate scoped work already included once in the total feasibility line.

The artist-fee envelope allocates $120,000 to six signature briefs, $30,000 to 12 direct-artist salon stipends and $50,000 to paid teaching/talk/performance/community work. The production/fit-out budget covers technical work separately from artist fees. Budget revisions follow real riders and quotes.

## Funding and liquidity

Stage one seeks **$75,000 paid discovery underwriting**: $70,000 work plus $5,000 reserve. The model counts the discovery amount once within sponsorship if the parties agree to that credit. It is not enough to mount the event.

The core scenario produces $265,000 before owner participation, tax provision and further cash reserves. The stated cancellation and receipt holds create a **$530,000 preopening bridge requirement**. Unrestricted sponsor cash reduces that requirement to $215,000 in the model. Neither is a committed facility. Plan to secure a suitably structured **up-to-$600,000 working-capital/cancellation facility** or equivalent usable cash, with professional terms and actual finance cost. Financing is not operating revenue.

Public grants are $0 in the base plan. The earlier research documents closed/paused rounds and eligibility limits. A genuine eligible public component may seek restricted support later, but never count it as unrestricted founder/owner profit.

## Proposed host arrangement

Seek waived base rent for a defined approved footprint/window, a controlled services schedule and a **10–20% share of defined distributable event profit**. The model uses 15% for comparison. The parties have agreed no rate or basis. Document and fund incremental building services within the $100,000 allowance. Check for double counting.

The model core waterfall holds $60,000 for tax (a model cash reserve without a tax calculation), $100,000 operating reserve and $56,500 customer-claim reserve. This leaves $48,500 initially eligible. A hypothetical 15% owner share is $7,275. If the claims hold were later fully released without losses, the cumulative owner comparison becomes $15,750. No amount is a promised distribution. Fees already pay legitimate work. GMV does not produce founder riches automatically.

Host benefits should therefore emphasize controlled activation, useful leasing interest and neighborhood identity alongside honest profit participation. The primary interview supports current rental intent. See the [preceding research](../../Summer-2027-Fair-Research/01-Developer-Rentals-and-Existing-Events.md). No condo-sale lead claim is appropriate.

## Delivery, governance and entity structure

Recommended structure to examine with counsel: a clearly accountable commercial event entity, contractual rights to the brand/digital assets and separate agreements with owner, producer, sellers, artists and underwriters. The platform/event relationship and founder ownership remain unresolved. A real charitable program may work with an appropriate nonprofit/fiscal sponsor. Do not manufacture a charity to finance private distributions.

The executive producer controls delivery and site interfaces. Artistic direction controls selection subject to conflicts. Finance controls funds and settlement. Product/program coordinates requirements. Brand controls identity. Specialist leads control their professional domains. Safety stop authority must be independent of commercial pressure.

Base weekly decisions on the hours and responsibilities people have accepted. Before money moves, the team must agree approval limits, related-party fees, conflicts, invoice evidence and dual review of cash transfers.

## Milestones and kill criteria

G0 November 15: funded discovery. G1 December 15: site feasibility. G2 January 31: accepted delivery organization. G3 March 31: launch funding/site evidence. G4 May 31: rights/funded program. G5 July 1: build authorization. G6 August 1: operational readiness. G7 August 8: open/no-open. G8 October 14: sixty-day close.

These internal target dates create no official filing deadline. If site phasing, accessible approved use, professional team or funded obligations fail, resize, relocate or cancel before exposure escalates. Revise the contained model until its revenue can cover its costs before selecting it. It is not an automatically profitable fallback. International stars and the concourse are never critical dependencies.

## Success, reporting and next edition

Measure completed art value, net seller receipts where consented, artist fees, distribution of artist outcomes, conversion, settlement speed, repeat-buyer intent, useful public access, incidents, sponsor delivery and unrestricted cash. Count entries and estimated unique visitors separately.

The year-one calendar covers November 1, 2026–October 31, 2027. A separate November 13 ninety-day claims/reserve review follows. A second edition requires sales and operating evidence, a usable site and new funding authority.
