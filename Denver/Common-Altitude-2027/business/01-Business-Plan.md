# COMMON ALTITUDE 2027 — proposed business plan

**Version 0.1 · October 24, 2026.** A complete working plan for a conditional first edition, not an approved event, financial forecast or offer. All commitments remain unmade. The governing numerical inputs are [project.json](../data/project.json) and [generated finance](../data/financial-results.md).

## Executive proposition

Create a seven-day art fair/exhibition in a carefully approved Denver adaptive-reuse site: **COMMON ALTITUDE / OFFICE OF THE IMPOSSIBLE**. Two professional/preview days lead into five free core-art discovery days, with optional paid special experiences and workshops. Serious buying service and exceptional public programming share one coherent identity.

The proposed planning week is **August 9–15, 2027**, with public art days August 11–15. No venue/date hold exists. The researched owner's conversion construction overlaps summer 2027, so site feasibility is the first commercial dependency. The two-tower/hundreds-of-rooms concept remains the expanded ambition; the recommended costed planning unit is **60 programmed rooms or equivalent spaces**, with 24- and 120-space alternatives.

The purpose is to generate completed art purchases and paid creative work that help sustain artists, while expanding the audience for art. The event supports the wider regional platform mission; it does not replace Taos-only website v1 or authorize a new website build.

## Market context and positioning

The Art Basel/UBS annual report estimates global 2025 art sales of $59.6bn, up 4%. This establishes a substantial but uneven market, not a Denver serviceable market or this event's revenue. The 2025 HNW collecting survey shows the relevance of galleries, fairs and artist-direct channels within its selected sample. Use those insights to design trust and service, not to transplant spending averages into a forecast. [Annual report](https://www.artbasel.com/news/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026), [dated collector channels](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/survey-of-global-collecting-2025/channels)

Denver already has respected arts festivals, galleries, museums, digital/public-art programs and immersive attractions. The gap to test is a coherent combination of an edited acquisition market, a remarkable multi-room exhibition, paid local participation and exceptional buying service at a useful scale. Do not claim the city lacks art or that established events need replacing.

The positioning is contemporary and full-spectrum, not limited to a Southwest style. First edition credibility comes from the quality of selected work, verified seller authority, the producer's competence and actual collector demand. Art Basel is a standard for discipline, not an affiliation or a plausible first-year status claim.

Actual acceptance and availability are unknown. Signature commissions/loans may be additional and not for sale. Do not total scraped asking prices into claimed inventory value.

## Revenue and artist economics

The proposal assumes **zero organizer commission on artwork sales**. Artist/gallery representation, payment processing, tax, shipping and external seller costs remain separate and disclosed. This is a proposed physical-fair structure, not already agreed festival policy. The existing direct-artist website zero-commission promise remains intact.

| Core organizer income | Assumption | Proposed gross |
|---|---|---:|
| Sponsorship | 17-package illustration, subject to actual demand and rights | $1,250,000 |
| Premium programs | 6,000 unique paid-program buyers × $50 average, within 12,000 total unique visitors | $300,000 |
| Optional gallery services | 24 purchases × $7,500 | $180,000 |
| Paid workshops | 1,000 paid seats × $35; workshop buyers may overlap premium buyers | $35,000 |
| Other earned services/publications | Unvalidated service/publication income placeholder | $50,000 |
| **Organizer income** | Excludes artwork seller balances, tax and restricted funds | **$1,815,000** |

Core gallery/artist selection is independent of payment. Optional services must have demonstrable value; the model's 100% uptake by 24 galleries is a strong unvalidated assumption. Define included handling, documentation or concierge support precisely. Participation cannot secretly become a mandatory fee to make the model work.

Core art target: **600 qualified collector households × 25% completed-purchase conversion × $20,000 average completed order = $3m**. Assume one order per purchasing household for this illustration. This is not a proven buyer list or forecast. Attendance, deposits and asking prices are not completed art GMV.

## Costs and pay

The proposed core cost envelope is **$1,550,000**, including $70,000 paid feasibility. It contains $260,000 core-team fees, $200,000 artist/program fees, $290,000 production/fit-out, $100,000 building operations, $120,000 art logistics, $100,000 visitor/security/access, $180,000 marketing/collector/PR, $65,000 professional/insurance/finance allowance and $165,000 contingency. Obtain quotes before commitment.

Illustrative later core-team allocations are artistic leadership $75,000, product/program $75,000, fractional brand $35,000, executive producer $55,000 and fractional finance $20,000. These are scope envelopes, not market-rate salaries, accepted appointments or employee classifications. They do not purchase unlimited hours. Feasibility fees are separate scoped work already included once in the total feasibility line.

The artist-fee envelope allocates $120,000 to six signature briefs, $30,000 to 12 direct-artist salon stipends and $50,000 to paid teaching/talk/performance/community work. Technical production of works is in production/fit-out, not hidden inside artist fees. Budget revisions follow real riders and quotes.

## Funding and liquidity

Stage one seeks **$75,000 paid discovery underwriting**: $70,000 work plus $5,000 reserve. It is credited once within later sponsorship if agreed. It is not enough to mount the event.

The core scenario produces $265,000 before owner participation, tax provision and further cash reserves. It also shows a peak **$530,000 preopening bridge requirement** under the stated cancellation/receipt holds, or $215,000 if sponsor cash were fully unrestricted. Neither is a committed facility. Plan to secure a suitably structured **up-to-$600,000 working-capital/cancellation facility** or equivalent usable cash, with professional terms and actual finance cost. Financing is not operating revenue.

Public grants are $0 in the base plan. The earlier research documents closed/paused rounds and eligibility limits. A genuine eligible public component may seek restricted support later, but never count it as unrestricted founder/owner profit.

## Proposed host arrangement

Seek waived base rent for a defined approved footprint/window, a controlled services schedule and a **10–20% share of defined distributable event profit**. The model uses 15% for comparison; no rate/basis is agreed. Incremental building services must be documented and funded without double counting the $100,000 allowance.

The illustrative core waterfall holds $60,000 for tax (placeholder, not a calculated liability), $100,000 operating reserve and $56,500 customer-claim reserve. This leaves $48,500 initially eligible; a hypothetical 15% owner share is $7,275. If the claims hold were later fully released without losses, the cumulative owner comparison becomes $15,750. No amount is a promised distribution. Fees already pay legitimate work; GMV does not produce founder riches automatically.

Host benefits should therefore emphasize controlled activation, useful leasing interest and neighborhood identity alongside honest profit participation. Current rental intent is supported by the primary interview in the [preceding research](../../Summer-2027-Fair-Research/01-Developer-Rentals-and-Existing-Events.md); no condo-sale lead claim is appropriate.

## Delivery, governance and entity structure

Recommended structure to examine with counsel: a clearly accountable commercial event entity, contractual rights to the brand/digital assets and separate agreements with owner, producer, sellers, artists and underwriters. The platform/event relationship and founder ownership remain unresolved. A real charitable program may work with an appropriate nonprofit/fiscal sponsor; do not manufacture a charity to finance private distributions.

The executive producer controls delivery and site interfaces; artistic direction controls selection subject to conflicts; finance controls funds and settlement; product/program coordinates requirements; brand controls identity; specialist leads control their professional domains. Safety stop authority must be independent of commercial pressure.

Weekly decisions use actual accepted capacity, not a paper org chart. Purchase/contract approval limits, related-party fees, conflicts, invoice evidence and dual review of cash movements must be agreed before money flows.

## Milestones and kill criteria

G0 November 15: funded discovery. G1 December 15: site feasibility. G2 January 31: accepted delivery organization. G3 March 31: launch funding/site evidence. G4 May 31: rights/funded program. G5 July 1: build authorization. G6 August 1: operational readiness. G7 August 8: open/no-open. G8 October 14: sixty-day close.

Dates are internal targets, not official filing deadlines. If site phasing, accessible approved use, professional team or funded obligations fail, resize, relocate or cancel before exposure escalates. The contained model must first be made viable; it is not an automatically profitable fallback. International stars and the concourse are never critical dependencies.
