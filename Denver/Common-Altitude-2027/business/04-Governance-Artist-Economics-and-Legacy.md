# Governance, artist economics and durable benefit

**Proposed operating principles, October 24, 2026.** This plan creates no entity, ownership split, compensation agreement or festival authority.

## Decision rights

A small accountable founding group sets mission and budget direction. The executive producer owns integrated delivery and site operations. Qualified specialists retain authority over safety/access/engineering. Artistic direction owns selection with recusals. Finance controls usable cash and settlement. Brand/product own their defined domains. Use a written approval matrix and private accepted-role register.

Before significant contracts: establish legal entity/counterpart authority, brand/IP rights, founder ownership/vesting and departure terms, related-party fee approval, banking/signatory controls, insurance, data responsibilities and dispute/escalation processes. An event profit share is not company equity.

## Artist proposition

The plan proposes free core participation for direct artists. Pay commissioned artists and educators for the work their contracts cover. The plan proposes zero organizer art commission. Galleries retain their own disclosed representation relationships. No promise overrides them. Payment-processing/tax/shipping/insurance responsibilities must be explicit.

Twelve proposed direct-artist salons share a $30,000 stipend envelope. Selection is independent of payment. This budget does not establish an open grant or guaranteed payment. Do not recruit unpaid production labor under the artist-opportunity banner.

Use clear artist/loan/consignment terms: scope, fee/expenses, work authority, rights, credits, condition/value, installation/handling, sales responsibility, cancellation, return and settlement. Keep artist and seller balances separate from organizer operating cash.

## Independence and culture

Disclose conflicts. A gallery adviser recuses from favoring represented artists. A sponsor does not select artists or suppress legitimate content by default. Cultural representation is artist/community-led. Indigenous, Latin American or local identity is not decorative theme material. Seek relevant permission and paid advice.

Institutions are not content warehouses. Museum/education participation requires mission/authority review, separate artist rights and appropriate fees. A local public consultation is not a substitute for consent from the people represented.

## Public benefit and legacy

Budget and measure free core art access, paid local learning and creative work, first-buyer support, accessible routes, repeat buyer relationships and completed artist payments.

Possible additional acquisition funds, grants, studio-support programs or year-round commissions need separate lawful funding, governance and accounting. They are not included as automatic deductions from art sales or invented tax-deductible donations. A sponsor’s art purchase pays the seller. Exclude it from organizer income unless a separate service contract earns a fee. Count completed GMV once and preserve independent selection.

## Compensation and upside

Defined funded scopes first, optional properly defined upside later. Founder service fees, ownership, staff pay, vendor fees, sponsorship-sales incentives and venue participation have distinct bases. Avoid stacking percentages against undefined “proceeds.”

The current core-fee and waterfall illustrations are in the [finance plan](03-Finance-and-Funding.md). Founders have agreed no split. Appropriate labor/tax/legal advice determines actual classification and distributions. Do not treat an employee as a volunteer or contractor merely because the plan is early-stage. [Colorado classification guidance](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%2310_who_is_and_isn%27t_an_employee_08.11.25.pdf)

## Accountability

Publish only cleared aggregate outcomes: completed art value, artist fees, distribution of sales outcomes, seller/customer service, access, participation, incidents and financial viability. Keep personal records private. Record failures and corrections as well as successes.

Use results from this edition to decide whether to propose another. It does not follow automatically from a brand launch, a large crowd or an ambitious deck.
