# Twenty experiments worth testing

**Creative R&D, not an approved program or expenditure.** These are original combinations proposed for this event, not claims that no one has ever tried similar ideas. Keep only the experiments that serve artists, audiences and actual buying confidence. None depends on an unconfirmed celebrity.

## The three strongest additions to investigate

**1. The building becomes a collector.** Instead of using art only to advertise future housing/office space, invite the host and appropriate design clients to acquire or commission work for the completed development. A temporary fair becomes a serious acquisition encounter with a long-term destination for art. Use a separate approved acquisition budget and independent selection; owner purchases are seller transactions, not organizer operating income or guaranteed event revenue. Artists keep authorship and actual terms. Test with a small qualified design/acquisition brief before proposing a fund.

**2. The collector's missing room.** A quiet, beautiful appointment space helps someone imagine a work in their life. Movable neutral panels, material/light samples, a scale reference and a knowledgeable person are enough for an early prototype. A buyer may voluntarily share a wall plan or image, kept privately and deleted under policy. Do not start with expensive AR or infer private wealth. Measure whether the experience answers a genuine buying question.

**3. An intercity room exchange.** Invite a small number of excellent galleries/curators from different cities to each author one precise presentation, with a reciprocal future conversation or visit. Denver, Taos/Santa Fe and an appropriate outside city become connected through real work rather than a generic international logo list. Each party needs its own accepted rights, travel/freight, expenses and seller terms. A modest exceptional exchange may be more credible than an unattainable roster.

These are optional investigations. They are not already priced in as extra revenue, contracted commissions or approved additions to the footprint.

## Choose by value, not novelty volume

Score the prototype on artistic strength, useful buyer/public outcome, authorship, accessibility, funded scope, technical feasibility and maintenance. Do not pretend a speculative concept is an available work by a named artist.

Select at most a few optional experiments for the first edition and put each through the normal gates. Reuse the existing signature/marketing/service allocations only where scope truly fits; otherwise fund a separate approved change. A coherent event should not become twenty competing technology demonstrations.
