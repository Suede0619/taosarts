# Collector development, art sales and acquisition service

**Working proposal, October 24, 2026.** The $3m goal is an unvalidated target. The project has no confirmed buyer list, inventory, gallery commitment or sale.

## Reasons for collectors to attend

The collector offer needs specific appropriate work, credible selection, trusted sellers, good appointments and reliable delivery. A spectacular room can start a conversation. It cannot replace authenticity/condition information or seller competence.

The collector lead should work with artistic direction and actual sellers. Ask each buyer about their interests and relevant work. Corporate/design acquisition has different authority, timing and procurement needs from a private collection.

## Qualified-household target

A possible allocation of the 600-household target is 200 gallery-originated, 150 adviser-originated, 100 existing consensual relationships/referrals, 80 corporate/design and 70 appropriate organic enquiries. This allocation sets research tasks. It contains no existing database or permission to transfer client records.

Accepted galleries can invite through their own channels and offer buyer-controlled introductions. Institutions may choose to circulate an approved invitation. Their membership lists are not event property. Keep real identities, budgets, collection details and travel/access needs in a controlled private system.

## A practical pipeline

Research reference then authorized contact route then consented relevant conversation then qualified intent then appointment/viewing proposed then confirmed then attended/remote viewing then work/quote request then seller hold/offer then seller invoice/payment then fulfilled/completed then resolved or closed.

Record source, responsible person, next action/date, consent, relevant interests, private access requirements and actual stage evidence. A booked appointment is not a sale. A deposit is not completed GMV. Record reluctance, price concerns, delivery concerns and missing context to improve the service. Respect the buyer’s decision.

## Purchase-ready artwork

Before offering a specific work: confirm seller authority and representation, artist/title/year, medium/dimensions, price/currency and tax/shipping basis, condition, provenance/edition information, image rights and availability date. Complex authenticity or legal/tax questions go to qualified people. No AI certification or investment-return pitch.

Show whole-work images and useful scale. Provide details and context with credits. Offer a human conversation. A smaller work deserves the same care as an expensive one.

## Appointment experience

Confirm who is attending, interest, time zone/date, meeting point, access needs and seller/concierge owner. Allow time to look. Keep performance sound away from quiet appointments. If the work becomes unavailable, tell the buyer promptly and offer appropriate alternatives only with seller authority.

For remote buyers, use accurate images/video and a staffed appointment. Explain that screen settings affect color and that condition needs separate evidence. Agree shipment/installation/insurance responsibilities in the seller's actual terms.

## Seller-led transactions

The baseline uses the authorized seller's invoice/payment route and receipt. The plan proposes zero organizer commission. External processor, gallery representation, tax and shipping costs remain explicit. The event does not borrow seller money or hold a speculative central art-payment pool.

Use stable work/order references and buyer-consented attribution. Seller completion evidence can support aggregate reporting without copying private invoice/payment data into public Git. Use the seller and consumer dispute process in the actual agreement.

## After the fair

Set a staffed response schedule. Acknowledge inquiries, obtain a current seller response, provide full costs and record the buyer’s decision. Support delivery and installation, then confirm completion or record remaining obligations. Do not keep sending inventory after withdrawal of consent.

Report event-period completed sales and later attributed completed sales separately. Use a documented attribution window. The proposed ninety-day review is November 13. Deduplicate across gallery, artist, adviser and event channels.

## Collector-facing materials

The [collector deck](../decks/07-Collectors-and-Advisors.md) is an initial proposition. The eventual release catalogue needs real contracted work and rights. Helpful pieces include an appointment confirmation, full-cost quote checklist, artwork-care guide, delivery handoff and first-collection explainer.

The museum/curator/adviser network is not a guaranteed buyer pipeline. The proposal promises no commission for transferring private clients. Any commercial incentive needs clear payer, basis, attribution, refunds and conflict review.
