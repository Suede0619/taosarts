# Marketing plan

**Working plan, October 24, 2026.** Dates/site/partners and every demand metric remain unvalidated. Public activation follows G3 and the relevant permissions. This document does not authorize messages, purchases or advertising. The [daily action plan](../operations/DAILY-ACTION-PLAN.md) is the dated execution source.

## Objectives and priorities

1. Attract qualified people who actually acquire art. Target $3m completed art GMV with protected seller economics.
2. Make the public invitation clear and welcoming. Target 12,000 unique visitors, with entries/estimation methods separately reported.
3. Validate and sell useful optional programs. The core model assumes 6,000 premium-program buyers at $50 average and 1,000 workshop seats at $35.
4. Recruit art and program partners who meet the selection criteria.
5. Win and fulfill cash underwriting. Target $1.25m, with no in-kind value substituted for cash.
6. Build durable artist/buyer relationships and evidence for a possible second edition.

These targets lack demand validation. Priority order is site/team/funding credibility then appropriate work and collectors then truthful public discovery then paid-program conversion then evidence-led retention.

## Positioning and audience strategy

**Positioning:** extraordinary artist-authored rooms, a trusted art market and a public welcome. The event should feel worth a collector's trip and comfortable for a resident's first visit.

| Segment | Actual need | Proposition / primary channel | Proof before scaling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Denver/Front Range collectors | Quality, trust and relevant work | Gallery/adviser introductions, appointments and useful content | Qualified conversations and credible seller inventory |
| Wider Southwest/Mountain buyers | A reason to travel and a workable itinerary | Selected galleries, advisers, hotels and regional art relationships | Actual intent, dates and service requirements |
| North American/international buyers | Exceptional work and professional confidence | Seller/adviser-led invitations and specific editorial interest | Appropriate confirmed artists/work. Not generic city hype |
| Corporate/hospitality/design buyers | Work fitting real projects and procurement | Scoped acquisition briefs through qualified advisers | Budget, authority, timing and conflict disclosure |
| First buyers/residents | Welcome, prices and easy help | Local arts/community partners, clear public information and learning | User comprehension and useful free route |
| Creative audiences/students/families | Participation and access | Paid educators, local artists, community organizations and relevant media | Safeguarding, access and actual program availability |

The geographic priorities need demand research. Begin with relationships where real credibility exists. Do not buy private wealth/contact dossiers or treat museum membership as permission to use a list.

## Channel system

**Partner-led:** accepted galleries/artists invite relevant people through their own authorized channels. Provide licensed co-marketing assets, accurate practical information and an appropriate referral route. Do not require private client-list transfer.

**Collector/adviser:** the collector lead handles useful individual invitations, appointments and follow-through. Track the cost, scope and resulting demand from small paid or hosted conversations.

**Owned:** an accurate catalogue, clear event/program pages, editorial, consented email and practical arrival/access information. Every page should answer the next useful question. Interest-list signup is not a ticket sale.

**Earned:** a specific artistic story with credible access to approved artists, process and site imagery. “The world's biggest” and unconfirmed celebrity hints are poor substitutes for news.

**Paid:** test search/social/local media after the conversion and refund paths work. Use consent-compliant audiences and licensed assets. Stop weak campaigns before spending the full allowance.

**On-site/post-event:** excellent labels, informed staff, appointments, seller-led full-cost quotes and delivery support. The most persuasive campaign can be the quality of the purchase experience.

## Budget and accountability

The proposed $180,000 marketing/collector/PR envelope is:
- Collector development/service: $60,000.
- Sponsorship development: $25,000.
- PR/editorial relations: $30,000.
- Paid media: $45,000.
- Content/campaign asset production: $20,000.

The budget allocates fees and spending by scope. The project has no salary quotes or reach guarantees. Core-team fees cover a separate brand and product scope. Avoid double counting.

Model paid-media split: $10k high-intent search, $20k selected social, $8k appropriate retargeting only after privacy review and $7k local placements/tests. Start with a $5k learning tranche inside the allowance. Actual results determine continuation.

Attributing 2,500 unique visitors to paid media would produce a modeled cost of $18 per visitor. If half purchased a premium product, cost would be $36 per paid-program buyer before other costs. These calculations test assumptions. Actual results must establish performance and margin. Free public visits also have mission value. Do not disguise their acquisition cost as ticket ROI.

## Funnel and measurable targets

| Milestone | Demand evidence to aim for | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 15 | 30 useful buyer/adviser interviews | Qualitative research. It cannot validate $3m in demand |
| Jan 31 | 100 qualified-household records. Eight credible local gallery anchors under appropriate conditional terms | Consenting prospects who have expressed interest |
| Mar 31 | 250 qualified households. 16 appropriate seller prospects/conditional participants | Public launch also needs funding/site gates |
| May 31 | 24 accepted gallery presentations and rights/funded program evidence | No purchased selection. Optional services priced separately |
| July 1 | 600 qualified households and 300 confirmed appointments | Attendance/remote viewing intent, fit and availability checked |
| Event/attribution window | 150 deduplicated purchasing households / $20k aggregate average target | Completed transactions only. Late sales separately dated |

Qualify a household only after a responsible lead confirms contact permission, art interests, self-described buying intent and timing. Budget disclosure is optional. Do not infer wealth from protected/sensitive traits.

One way to test the $3m aggregate is 120 gallery purchasing households averaging $22,500 and 30 direct-artist households averaging $10,000. These allocations divide the existing target. They add no sales forecast. Deduplicate households/orders across sellers and referrals. Do not add edition sales again without actual completed records.

## Campaign phases

**Nov–Dec: credibility and listening.** Finish site/code discovery, paid scopes, concept testing, seller value and buyer needs. No unsupported public launch.

**Jan–Mar: build the proposition.** Contract/conditional partner work as authorized, underwriting, inventory standards, prototype buying journey and useful content. Prepare launch evidence and a truthful press story.

**Apr–May: controlled announcement and service readiness.** Only after G3, release approved dates/site status and actual participants. Test registration, free/paid choices, access information and customer support before paid reservations.

**Jun–Jul: conversion.** Confirm work, appointments and travel. Publish useful artist/process/context pieces. Test paid channels. Show practical value. Every famous name remains absent from public roster until contracted and permitted.

**Aug: service and delivery.** Useful reminders, live factual updates, artist/seller storytelling and buyer care. Reconcile results daily. No manufactured crowd or sales claims.

**Sep–Oct: follow-through.** Deliver purchases, resolve issues, support actual remaining buyer interest, report cleared aggregates and evaluate renewal. Respect unsubscribe/retention choices.

## Experiment register

| Test | Minimum useful evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| $50 guided journey | Representative users understand and value the actual proposed service. Paid/conditional research appropriate to authority | Reprice/reduce income assumptions if value or uptake fails |
| Optional $7,500 gallery services | Specific deliverables, cost and seller willingness under clear terms | Do not force fees or assume full uptake |
| Collector travel | Actual relevant work, appointment interest and travel constraints | Change roster/date/service before buying reach |
| Creative direction | Visitors understand art/market/public invitation and recall the distinctive idea | Use feedback to improve the design and test it again |
| Paid acquisition | Qualified conversions and customer value with privacy-compliant attribution | Stop weak spend. Avoid optimizing for empty impressions |
| Informed purchase | A complete tested enquiry-to-seller-payment-to-delivery process | Fix service failures before scaling traffic |

## Reporting rhythm

Weekly: qualified pipeline, accepted work/rights, funding cash/commitments, campaign spend, conversion, service defects and upcoming gate. Daily during show: access/queue/service issues, reservations, actual seller-reported completed orders and customer care. Post-event: reconciled art/artist/public/sponsor outcomes.

Use one private CRM and finance ledger. Public Git contains approved aggregates and plans, never real buyer records or confidential sponsor pipeline. The marketer cannot override artistic independence, site approval, refund obligations or privacy.
