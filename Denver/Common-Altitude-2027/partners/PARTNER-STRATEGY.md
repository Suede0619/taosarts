# Art-supply and program partnership strategy

**Working research/invitation strategy · October 24, 2026.** [Denver](Denver/README.md), [North America](North-America/README.md) and [worldwide](Worldwide/README.md) each contain 24 uncontacted candidates. This is a research longlist, not a roster or assertion of availability.

## Four different propositions

1. **Gallery/seller presentation:** appropriate available work, seller authority, independent selection and clear optional service terms. The gallery retains its representation relationships and seller responsibilities.
2. **Artist commission/loan:** a paid artist-authored scope or an authorized loan with real rights, handling, insurance, technical and return terms.
3. **Institution/education/community program:** a mission-aligned, institution-approved paid scope. Museums, districts and nonprofits are not assumed art wholesalers or endorsers.
4. **Production/technical service:** a qualified paid vendor/producer with specific responsibility; a spectacular portfolio is not the same as art-market competence.

Never ask every candidate to “bring content.” A useful invitation explains why their actual practice fits, the artistic freedom, credible audience, fee/economics, site constraints, proposed timing and next decision.

## Target selection and evidence

The core design targets 24 gallery presentations and 12 direct-artist salons. A useful tentative sourcing mix is 12 Denver/Front Range galleries, six wider North American and six international-network presentations; revise based on quality, actual work and feasible cost. Do not force a geographic quota or represent a global gallery as agreed because it appears on the list.

Review artistic quality, specific room argument, purchase-ready work, pricing/context clarity, representation/rights, physical delivery, local/public contribution, availability and realistic costs. Do not assign a false numerical “likelihood to join” without conversations.

A partner is publicly included only after the required agreement and permission. Track research → authorized approach → interested → scoped/terms pending → contracted → funded/ready → delivered → reconciled, with source/evidence. Interest is not commitment; a commitment is not delivered work.

## Gallery and artist offer

Proposed zero organizer artwork commission; seller-led transactions and disclosed external costs; clear rights and condition care; independent curation; informed appointments; excellent public context; and appropriate production/service support.

The optional $7,500 gallery-service hypothesis must specify what is bought and what remains available without it. It cannot secretly become a selection charge. The model assumes 24 buyers of the service; test lower uptake. Direct-artist salon stipends and commissions are separate funded scopes.

Avoid an unfunded promise to pay artist travel, freight, technical build, meals and hotel for every participant. Obtain all-in proposals and assign each expense in the approved budget.

## Invitation brief to personalize privately

- Why this specific artist/gallery/organization fits.
- The original artistic/public/market proposition and actual status.
- What is being requested: advisory time, work, loan, room, commission, teaching or service.
- Proposed date/footprint and unresolved dependencies.
- Fee/economics, expenses, rights, artist authority and cancellation.
- What evidence/response would help the next decision.
- Responsible authorized counterpart and expected process.
- No use of their name/logo/work in public until permission.

Do not send generic mass invitations or disclose a private client list. External outreach remains unexecuted by this planning package.

## Supply-chain and curatorial fallback

Core success should be possible with excellent regional work plus selected feasible outside contributions. Replace unavailable global concepts with artist-approved alternatives, not replicas. Scale down rooms coherently rather than fill them with unselected stock, unlicensed images or unstaffed attractions.

The room count is a demand on production/curation, not a reason to accept everything. A few exceptional rooms and reliable transactions beat an incoherent building full of names.
