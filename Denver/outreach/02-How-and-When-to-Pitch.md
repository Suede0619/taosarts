# Denver: how and when to pitch

**October 25, 2026. Proposed communication sequence.** The current event is a [completed planning package](../Common-Altitude-2027/README.md), with feasibility and commitments still unresolved. A seven-day concept, a 60-space planning model and the suggested August 2027 week are assumptions. Do not present them as a booking or approved event.

## The first pitch is for the next useful decision

For an authorized property counterpart, the immediate objective is a **bounded feasibility conversation**. Establish who can decide, which part of the site is even possible, how construction affects use and what evidence the owner wants. The full festival vision supplies context. It should not substitute for these questions.

An appropriate opening is: “We have a preliminary art-event concept. Before asking for space or support, we'd like to understand whether any defined part of the property could be considered and what process you would require.”

Explain the public/artist purpose and possible owner benefit without promising rental uplift, financing, audience size or commercial results. The owner is entitled to say that the use does not fit.

## Persona-by-persona matrix

| Persona | Primary product or decision | Short pitch / question | When and next ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| DV01. Owner representative | A site-feasibility process | “Could a bounded, funded cultural use fit any part of the property without compromising its existing obligations?” | Invited business meeting. Confirm decision authority and the required feasibility inputs. |
| DV02. Property/construction manager | Operational compatibility | “What footprint and operating conditions, if any, could be examined without creating conflict with tenants or construction?” | Before an event footprint, capacity or public date claim. Ask for the actual site process and information needed. |
| DV03. Creative counterpart | Curatorial and portfolio fit | “What would an artist-centered buying/public program add to the cultural work already happening here?” | After permission to discuss. Ask for a critique of one contained program concept, not blanket affiliation. |
| DV04. Technical specialist | A paid feasibility scope | “We need professional review of an actual use and footprint. What information, access, scope and fee would that require?” | Before promising safe public use or production capacity. Engage through the proper funded process. |
| DV05. City/permit official | A process consultation | “Which approvals would apply to this specific proposed use, and which office should address each question?” | With concrete assumptions and an accountable applicant. No public claim of approval from an informal response. |
| DV06. Underwriter/funder | A staged feasibility or delivery budget | “Would you consider a defined stage with a clear budget, deliverables, risks and stop decision before the team commits to a larger event?” | A stage ask requires a responsible recipient/operator and costed scope. Never describe artist balances as financing. |
| DV07. Sponsor | A contracted public/client benefit | “What particular experience or audience outcome would be valuable to you? We can scope a benefit that can actually be delivered and measured.” | After program, rights and delivery costs exist. Do not sell unproved attendance or owner endorsement. |
| DV08. Arts/community partner | Paid local participation and access | “What would make this worthwhile to local artists and residents, and what would create burden or compete with existing work?” | Listening before final program design. No implied community consent or free staffing request. |
| DV09. Gallery | An acquisition/show opportunity | “If the site and edition are feasible, could a curated selling program with clear terms and competent collector service be useful to your gallery?” | Discovery may precede approval if labeled conditional. Formal recruitment waits for a credible deliverable edition. |
| DV10. Artist | A defined paid contribution | “What would a worthwhile paid contribution look like for you? We need to establish scope, rights and resources before requesting creative work.” | After a real brief and funding basis exist. No unpaid speculative production. |
| DV11. Collector | A relevant art encounter | “What kind of work or access would justify a visit? We'd like to understand that before promising a program.” | Permissioned buyer research first. A viewing invitation needs real work, sellers, location and dates. |
| DV12. Producer/supplier/crew | A funded scoped job | “Could you assess a defined production scope once the site facts are available, and identify the principal constraints and costs?” | After footprint/use assumptions are adequate for a responsible estimate. No job commitment implied. |
| DV13. Professional adviser | A specific review engagement | “We need advice on the actual entities, obligations and activity. What documents and engagement scope would you require?” | Before agreements and public claims that depend on the advice. A template is not an opinion. |
| DV14. Neighbor/tenant | An impact discussion | “What access or operating conflicts would need resolution for this to be acceptable?” | Early enough to change the plan. Return with a specific response and responsibility. |
| DV15. Media | A verifiable story | “There is an exploratory proposal. We'd be glad to share verified milestones when there is something real to report.” | A concept discussion is conditional. Announcement waits for the relevant permissions and cleared claims. |
| DV16. Hospitality/destination | A real event-linked guest service | “Once the event basis is established, could a small visitor-service collaboration be useful to your guests or business?” | After date/site facts and a defined service exist. No speculative room, booking or revenue promises. |

## Readiness gates

| Stage | A suitable conversation | Evidence before advancing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Internal alignment | Decide who wants to present and what they can truthfully explain. | Accurate roles, speaking consent, one concept summary and a list of unknowns. |
| 2. Recipient/fit discovery | Ask the proper counterpart whether and how the concept could be considered. | Identity, mandate, geography and an invited next step. |
| 3. Feasibility | Scope site, construction, public use, professionals and an accountable operator. | Actual information, professional process, paid work where required and a stage budget. |
| 4. Underwriting and participation | Seek defined support or conditional interest with explicit dependencies. | A feasible edition, credible economics, terms, rights and adequate delivery capacity. |
| 5. Contracting and announcement | Make commitments or publish dates only with the relevant actual authority. | Appropriate approvals, funding, responsibilities and permission for each claim. |

No fixed outreach date follows from project day one on November 1. The reported construction overlap makes site fit an early decision. A full ambition deck can be shared for invited review, clearly marked as a proposal. It cannot turn a later gate into a completed one.

## Social timing and presentation behavior

At another artist's opening, focus on the work and conversation. If someone asks, give a one-sentence description and ask whether they would prefer to continue another time. Do not introduce a companion as an automatic presenter. An agreed short introduction is not permission for an unscheduled presentation.

In a booked meeting, ask what the recipient needs to decide. Present the relevant page, not the entire deck by default. Name the next decision, the unanswered questions and the proposed follow-up. Record permission accurately. One invited reminder at an appropriate interval is enough before closing the loop.

## AI in the Denver pitch

AI-assisted planning can help create drafts, compare options and organize records. Owner confidence will depend on professional competence, funding, safe site use and accountable execution. Do not describe generated renders as actual approved space, simulated attendance as a forecast or planning software as a substitute for a producer or licensed professional.

If asked, explain which materials used AI, what people checked and what remains a proposal. Do not upload private contracts, portfolios, site-sensitive records or personal data without permission and an appropriate configuration.
