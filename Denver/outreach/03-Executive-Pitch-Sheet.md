# Denver: executive pitch sheet by audience

**October 25, 2026. Proposed scripts after permission to explain.** These target a calm 25–35 seconds. Evaluation is editorial judgment, not tested recipient response. The [full matrix](02-How-and-When-to-Pitch.md#persona-by-persona-matrix) lists every persona. All site, date, program, financing and participation claims remain conditional.

| Audience / personas | Primary product | Thirty-second version | Evaluation and next decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authorized owner and property team. DV01/02 | A bounded feasibility process | “We have a preliminary idea for an art event that could bring paid artist activity, collectors and a welcoming public program into a carefully defined space. Before discussing a large footprint or dates, we'd like to understand whether any part of the property could be considered without conflicting with construction, tenants or other obligations. What process would you want us to follow?” | Appropriate first owner conversation. It asks for process, not an unearned venue commitment. |
| Creative/curatorial counterpart. DV03 | A distinctive contained program | “We're exploring a curated art event where serious buying service and public discovery support each other. The value would come from the selected work, paid artist contributions and a coherent experience, not the size of the building alone. We'd like your view of what would add something worthwhile to existing activity before refining the program or claiming any affiliation.” | Good fit discussion. Needs an actual curatorial example and respect for existing programs. |
| Technical professionals and city interfaces. DV04/05/13 | A specific feasibility/review scope | “This is a concept that needs review against actual site conditions and the proposed use. We want to identify the right professional and agency processes before promising public access, capacity or dates. We can organize the assumptions and missing information, but we need to understand the scope, documents, access and funding required for a responsible next step.” | Clear about limits. Does not seek a professional opinion from a casual conversation. |
| Underwriter or anchor funder. DV06 | A stage with a stop decision | “We are exploring an artist-centered event and want to fund the next defined stage responsibly. That means a bounded scope, accountable delivery, a clear cost and explicit reasons to stop if site or demand evidence fails. We'd like to discuss whether that kind of staged support fits your purpose before asking anyone to back the full ambition.” | Credible only with a real stage budget/operator. No event-income guarantee. |
| Sponsor. DV07 | A useful contracted benefit | “If the event proves feasible, we'd like sponsorship to fund something useful and specific: a public program, paid artist work or a relevant client experience. We would define what you receive, what it costs to deliver and how fulfillment is checked. We are not offering an audience or association that has not been established. What outcome would matter to you?” | Appropriate after scope/rights exist. Never substitute logos for measurable value. |
| Galleries and artists. DV09/10 | A fair art-selling or paid program opportunity | “The purpose is to create meaningful art encounters and paid outcomes for artists, with a serious service process for buyers. The venue and edition are still conditional, so this is an early fit conversation. Before inviting participation, we'd need clear scope, selling and payment terms, rights and delivery responsibility. What would make an opportunity like that worthwhile for you?” | Honest discovery. Recruitment requires a feasible funded edition and actual terms. |
| Community partners, neighbors and producers. DV08/12/14 | A locally workable event | “We want the proposal to be useful to local artists and residents and workable for the people who live, work and produce around it. That means paid responsibilities, realistic access and a response to practical conflicts. We'd like to understand those needs before fixing the format. What would we have to get right for you to support a next discussion?” | Listening must change the plan. A conversation is not community approval or supplier commitment. |
| Collectors and hospitality partners. DV11/16 | A credible art visit | “We're exploring an event built around selected work, informed introductions and dependable service. Before promising a visit, we'd like to understand what art and experience would justify your time. The site, dates and participants are not confirmed. Your feedback could help define a useful edition, and any invitation or guest-service collaboration would follow real arrangements.” | Useful buyer/host research. No date or booking pressure. |
| Media. DV15 | A factual local story | “There is a developing proposal for an art event in an adaptive-use setting. We're still resolving site feasibility, delivery and funding. We'd welcome an independent look when there are verified milestones, actual participants and cleared material to discuss. We would rather provide accurate evidence and open questions than imply that a concept is already an approved event.” | Protects credibility. Public announcements follow facts and permissions. |

**A social-event sentence, only if asked:** “We're exploring an artist-centered Denver event, with site feasibility and local collaboration still to work through.”

If the person is interested: “Would another time be better for a short conversation?” Return attention to the current event and its hosts.
