# Four Denver towers — acquisitions, budgets and conversion timeline

**As of October 24, 2026. USD; nominal figures.** Amounts below describe property acquisitions, proposed development costs and public-loan actions. They are not an event budget, cash on hand or proof of financing close. Sources vary by date and scope; preserve those differences.

## The two transactions

| Complex | Buildings / addresses | Reported acquisition | Published scale | Proposed use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High Fidelity Plaza** | **621 and 633 17th Street**, Upper Downtown | **$3.2m**, reported April 4, 2025 | Roughly 970,000–1m square feet across two towers and shared facilities | More than 700 homes plus retail, public/plaza activity and amenities. Exact plan counts vary by submission. |
| **Denver Energy Center** | **1625 and 1675 Broadway**, by 16th Street | Auction/contract publicized September 2025; later reporting places acquisition in **December 2025**; **$5.25m**, often rounded to **$5.3m** | Owner publishes approximately **785,000 sq ft**; some reporting uses around **900,000 sq ft** | Current reporting places **386 apartments at 1625**; **1675 remains office/commercial**, with amenity upgrades. |

Sources: [High Fidelity acquisition report](https://old.businessden.com/2025/04/04/downtown-towers-sell-for-3m-700-unit-residential-conversion-planned/), [September auction/contract account](https://www.denvergazette.com/outtherecolorado/2025/09/19/this-developer-will-soon-own-4-denver-skyscrapers-the-plan-convert-them/), [Energy Center owner page](https://www.luzzatto.company/housing/1625-1675-broadway), [July 2026 Energy Center account](https://businessden.com/luzzatto-asks-ddda-for-29m-for-second-residential-conversion/).

The two reported purchase amounts total **$8.45m** using $3.2m + $5.25m. This is arithmetic from reported consideration, **not an audited all-in acquisition basis** including debt, fees, reserves, carrying costs, ground leases or capital work. The press story concerns **four towers**, not four separately documented transactions.

Energy Center’s $5.25m auction figure was about **97% below the reported $176m 2013 sale**. These are transactions twelve years apart, not an appraisal of current value or a realized return for the new buyer. The headline discount does not remove the redevelopment costs. [September 2025 report](https://www.denvergazette.com/outtherecolorado/2025/09/19/this-developer-will-soon-own-4-denver-skyscrapers-the-plan-convert-them/).

## Development cost and public financing are separate figures

| Project / figure | Amount | Date / evidence | Correct interpretation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| High Fidelity early development estimate | $150m–$200m | April 2025 reporting | Early scope estimate, superseded by later/larger project figures; not current committed budget. |
| High Fidelity city-published estimated project cost | **$315m** | March 25, 2026 city announcement | Total estimated development cost at that date, not acquisition price or money already spent. |
| High Fidelity DDDA action | **Up to $63m low-interest loan approved by DDDA board** | March 25, 2026 | Approval is not disbursement. Executed terms, senior financing, draw conditions and current balance were not reviewed. |
| Energy Center application total | **$145m** | July 13, 2026 report describing application | Application-stage total; do not add it to the later $150m figure. |
| Energy Center current reported total estimate | **Approximately $150m** | September 14, 2026 developer reporting | Later project estimate, not a certified final guaranteed price. |
| Energy Center permit/log construction valuation | **Approximately $62m** | September report of July 31 log | A reported construction-valuation field, not interchangeable with the whole project’s budget. |
| Energy Center requested DDDA support | **$29m requested** | July/September 2026 coverage | A request. An approval/disbursement was not established in the reviewed evidence. |

Sources: [April acquisition/estimate](https://old.businessden.com/2025/04/04/downtown-towers-sell-for-3m-700-unit-residential-conversion-planned/), [city announcement](https://www.denvergov.org/News-articles/2026/DDDA-Approves-Funding-for-High-Fidelity-Plaza-Downtown-Safety-Action-Plan), [application account](https://businessden.com/luzzatto-asks-ddda-for-29m-for-second-residential-conversion/), [permit/developer report](https://www.bisnow.com/news/denver/multifamily/developer-applies-for-construction-permit-for-downtown-denver-office-to-residential-project).

Using $315m and $145m–$150m produces **$460m–$465m of published development-cost estimates across two projects**. This is a comparison of dated estimates, not a verified consolidated capital plan, fully funded investment, event-sponsorship capacity or an amount to add to the properties’ old sale prices. Do not double-count acquisition costs when total-development-cost definitions have not been reconciled.

The city announcement identifies more than 700 High Fidelity apartments, at least 70 restricted at 60% AMI, and neighborhood-serving uses. It does **not** state that all apartments are legally income restricted. Early headlines using “affordable” broadly should not be converted into a fully affordable-housing claim. [City source](https://www.denvergov.org/News-articles/2026/DDDA-Approves-Funding-for-High-Fidelity-Plaza-Downtown-Safety-Action-Plan).

## Source conflicts that matter

| Conflict | Current treatment |
|---|---|
| Energy Center acquired in September versus December 2025 | September supports auction/contract activity; later acquisition reporting uses December. No deed was recovered to settle exact transfer date. Use “acquired in 2025,” with month qualification when relevant. |
| Tower number/address reversal | A February 2026 [Connect CRE report](https://www.connectcre.com/stories/discounted-denver-office-tower-candidate-for-massive-makeover/) reverses the use/address pairing relative to later July/September accounts. Use **1625 residential / 1675 office** as the current reported configuration; obtain owner plans. |
| 785,000 versus roughly 900,000 sq ft | Owner publishes 785,000; later reporting uses another total. These may differ in area definition; unreconciled. Do not price exhibition space or size capacity from either. |
| High Fidelity 700 / 712 / 750 units; Energy Center 350–400 / 360 / 386 / 500 units | Plans changed and reports use different scope/date. Current city High Fidelity description is “over 700”; current Energy Center reporting is 386. Do not combine the largest historical numbers into a new pipeline claim. |
| High Fidelity $310m / $315m / earlier $150m–$200m | Retain source/date/scope; use city’s dated $315m estimate for the main comparison. None is an audited final cost. |
| Atelier pages written as if transformation is complete | Treat as portfolio/design narrative. Contemporary permit/timing reporting does not establish completed residences. |
| “Received” financing versus board approval | For High Fidelity, a board loan action is verified; executed agreement, draws and current loan balance were not. For Energy Center, reviewed sources establish a request, not an award. |

## What the research does not settle

Current deed/affiliate ownership, recorded lender restrictions, final loan documents, issued permit numbers, current construction mobilization, floor-by-floor possession, tenant move-out dates, approved assembly use, exact completion schedules and the currently available event footprint were not independently obtained. These are the documents that would determine what a future event could actually occupy. No review of this research substitutes for the owner’s current plans and permission.
